**Length:** about 2:05 (1:50–2:10) · vertical 9:16 · YouTube Short · **Location:** Utsunomiya (Tochigi), Japan's "Gyoza City": the station, the Venus of Gyoza statue, then Chef Taka's small lantern-lit gyoza counter · **Dumpling:** Yaki-gyoza 焼き餃子 (pan-fried gyoza) · **Method:** pan-fried (fry, steam, crisp) · **Dad's journal clue:** Friendly rivals

Merged from the old Japan arc (`scripts/archive/old-04-gyoza.md`, `old-05-gyoza-culture.md`, `old-06-gyoza-recipe.md`): the frying-pan "knight" hook and ceiling gyoza (04), the three-sauce gag and the regional styles (05), and the "Dad's old pan" heart beat (06). Tokyo is now Utsunomiya. Chef Taka stays.

## Script

**[0:00–0:03] HOOK.** *SIZZLE!* Close-up: UNCLE holds a frying-pan lid like a knight's shield. Little oil drops pop behind it.
> **UNCLE:** It's ATTACKING me!

**[0:03–0:05] REWIND.** Freeze frame. Record-scratch, rewind swoosh.
> **BOY (V.O.):** Let's go back.

**[0:05–0:17] THE JOURNEY.** Dad's journal: the dotted line runs from **Kobe** east to Tokyo, then north to **Utsunomiya**. Two cartoon bullet trains zip along it. Inside, UNCLE hugs a paper bag of Kobe buns. He peeks in. Empty. He peeks in again, hopeful. Still empty.
> **BOY (V.O.):** Two bullet trains… Utsunomiya!
> **UNCLE:** *(whispering to the bag)* …I'm sorry.

**[0:17–0:20] TITLE CARD.** `🥟 #08 GYOZA · Utsunomiya, Japan`

**[0:20–0:36] ARRIVAL: GYOZA CITY.** Bright postcard shots, real sounds (station chime, bicycle bells, sizzling pans):
- Outside the station: a smooth stone statue of a goddess wrapped in a gyoza skin. UNCLE stops, removes an imaginary hat and bows very seriously.
> **UNCLE:** A hero.
- A street of small gyoza shops, one after another, short cloth curtains fluttering in every doorway. Steam and sizzle everywhere. UNCLE spins in a circle, not knowing which way to go.
> **BOY (V.O.):** In Utsunomiya, gyoza shops are everywhere.
> **UNCLE:** *(spinning)* I can't choose!

On-screen fact card: `🗿 Outside Utsunomiya Station stands the "Venus of Gyoza": a goddess wrapped in a gyoza, carved from local Ōya stone.`

**[0:36–0:58] THE GAG (pan).** The BOY spots the doodle from Dad's journal painted on one small sign: a pan and a gyoza. Inside, kind CHEF TAKA (60s, white headband, calm smile) slides over a plate of gyoza: golden-brown bottoms, soft pleated tops.
> **CHEF TAKA:** Irasshaimase! Welcome!
> **UNCLE:** I can do that. For science!

UNCLE grabs a pan of gyoza and flips it like a TV chef. *FWOOMP!* The gyoza fly up… and stick to the ceiling. Silence. One slowly peels off and drops right into his open mouth.
> **UNCLE:** *(chewing, proud)* …Chef's kiss.
> **CHEF TAKA:** *(gently)* Here… we don't flip.

Escalation: UNCLE tries a "tiny" flip of one gyoza with his chopsticks. It spins up and lands on his bald head like a little hat.
> **BOY:** *(trying not to laugh)* It suits you.
> **UNCLE:** *(dignified)* I meant that.

**[0:58–1:10] THE GAG (three sauces).** CHEF TAKA sets down a tiny dish. Three bottles light up on screen: `soy sauce` · `vinegar` · `a few drops of chili oil (rayu)`.
> **CHEF TAKA:** Mix your own.

Fast three-beat: (1) one drop of chili oil, UNCLE's face goes red, *poot poot* steam from his ears; (2) too much vinegar, his lips pucker into a fish mouth; (3) a bit of everything… his eyes go wide with joy.
> **UNCLE:** I invented a new sauce!
> **CHEF TAKA:** *(sniffs, smiles)* That's my sauce.

**[1:10–1:32] "MANY PEOPLE SAY…" / HISTORY.** Storybook-style animation (paper-cut look, warm colors). A Chinese jiaozi waves hello to a Japanese gyoza across a map.
> **BOY (V.O.):** Gyoza comes from China's jiaozi.
> **BOY (V.O.):** Many people say it took off after 1945…
> **BOY (V.O.):** …when families came home from northeast China, missing those dumplings.

Card: `Gyoza = Japan's name for jiaozi 🥟 · In Japan, usually pan-fried`

Map pop-up: `Utsunomiya 🥟` and `Hamamatsu 🥟`, two little cities waving gyoza flags and then high-fiving.
> **BOY (V.O.):** Around 1990, Utsunomiya proudly called itself "Gyoza City."
> **BOY (V.O.):** Hamamatsu says it too. Friendly rivals!

Quick card: `🎉 Every November, Utsunomiya holds a big gyoza festival.` UNCLE, in a dream bubble, waves from a gyoza-shaped float.

**[1:32–1:54] HEART MOMENT: DAD'S OLD PAN.** The lunch crowd thins. CHEF TAKA stops wiping the counter. He stares at the BOY's conical hat.
> **CHEF TAKA:** *(quietly)* That hat… I know it.

He brings out something wrapped in a cloth: a small, well-used cast-iron pan with a lid.
> **CHEF TAKA:** Your father cooked here. He asked me to keep this safe.
> **CHEF TAKA:** Now it's yours. *(small smile)* Never flip it.

The BOY holds the pan close, eyes closed, smiling. UNCLE turns away, wiping his eyes.
> **UNCLE:** *(sniffing)* Green onions. Very strong.

Sepia memory: DAD's silhouette (conical hat, from behind) and a young CHEF TAKA side by side at two stoves, laughing, racing to fry the crispiest gyoza.
> **BOY:** Dad wrote: *"A good rival makes your gyoza better. A good friend eats it with you."*

On the Utsunomiya page, a word glows in Dad's pen: `Friendly rivals.` ✨

**[1:54–2:05] BUTTON + CLIFFHANGER.** UNCLE stands on a stool, chopsticks stretched toward the one gyoza *still* stuck to the ceiling.
> **UNCLE:** We are NOT leaving that one.

The BOY lifts the pan's lid. Scratched inside, in Dad's handwriting: *"Yokohama. Keep it simple."*
> **BOY:** *(to camera)* Dad was right… Next: Yokohama! Crispy or steamed? 👇

## Accuracy notes
- **"Many people say"** is used for the 1945 boom: the broad point (postwar returnees from China made gyoza popular) appears in several reliable sources, but the detailed local story (which soldiers, which first shops) comes mostly from local and tourism accounts. Keep the WWII mention soft: "families came home". No war imagery.
- **"Around 1990":** sources agree the campaign followed the 1987 survey result and that the city was promoting the statistic by 1990; the exact start and who began it are not documented, so the script says "around 1990" and gives no names. The season map's "1990s" wording fits this.
- **Rivalry:** the household-spending ranking changes every year (Utsunomiya, Hamamatsu and recently Miyazaki have all been first), so no year's winner appears on screen. The two cities high-five; it's friendly, never mocking.
- **Venus statue:** drawn in storybook style. The Uncle's bow is about his enthusiasm, never mocking the statue. Its exact position (west exit) has changed with station works over the years, so the script only says "outside the station".
- **Festival:** say "every November" only, no dates or prices on screen. The parade float is the Uncle's daydream, not a claim about the real festival.
- **"Irasshaimase"** is everyday shop language, used warmly as texture.
- **Chef Taka** is fictional. His shop has no name on screen and no real signage. The only sign shown is Dad's doodle (a pan and a gyoza).
- **No recipe this episode:** the frying-pan "fry, steam, crisp" montage from the old Ep 06 now lives in Ep 3 (potstickers). This episode only shows the finished gyoza; the plate-flip and safety details are not needed here.
- **Story continuity:** Ep 7's doodle ("Taka is keeping my pan") sets up the pan. The note inside the lid ("Yokohama. Keep it simple.") sets up Ep 9, where the Boy steams shūmai in Dad's pan. The ceiling gyoza stays as a callback for Ep 9's last laugh.
