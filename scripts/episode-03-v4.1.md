## How to read the tables
- **Method:** `I2V` = image-to-video from one approved still (first frame; last frame where noted), 3-5 s, one action. `CODE` = paper-cut/map/graphics built locally. `FX` = edit effect over a still.
- Dialogue in curly quotes is spoken. `[CARD: ...]` is an on-screen graphic. Cuts and punch-ins inside a clip are made in the edit from the generated clip.
- **Recipe cards:** one grouped card per step (all amounts of the step on one card), each held at least 2.0 s; a small banner stays in the corner: `[CARD: 📝 Full recipe in the description]`. Safety badges use one look: red ⚠️ **GROWN-UP STEP**, blue 🧼 **RAW MEAT RULE**, yellow 🌾 **ALLERGY**. Helper hands appear only for squeezing, mixing, folding and pinching. If the edit runs long, trim the Uncle gags first, never a safety card.
- Times are clip start times (m:ss.t) and sum to exactly 2:00.0.

## Safety and sensitivity checks
- **Recipe safety (in the video and in the description):** every knife, hot pan, hot oil, splatter, doneness check and plate-flip step has a red GROWN-UP STEP badge on screen; the raw-meat rule (wash hands, never taste raw filling) and the allergy card (wheat, soy, sesame) are on screen and spoken; doneness is shown and spoken at pork 71 C / 160 F, chicken 74 C / 165 F (no pink, clear juices; "boil until they float" is NOT used as the test); "very hot inside, rest one minute" is on screen; the lid goes on right away and helpers stand back (the Uncle's fridge-door gag is the only fear gag, and the Boy and Mei model the calm, correct behavior). No coin in a dumpling (choking risk).
- **Filling amount fixed:** about 1 tablespoon per wrapper (the old "heaped teaspoon" would leave half the filling unused).
- **No fireworks:** Beijing has banned them. The midnight beat uses lantern light, snow, a clock and the murmur of a TV gala through the wall.
- **Legend hedged:** "Legend says" plus the asterisk ("folklore"); the doctor is a real physician, the dumpling story is folklore. No named emperor; the Song-dynasty "burned bottoms" palace-cook story is not used. The real-history beat says "dumplings over 1,300 years old", never "the oldest dumplings" (an older bronze-vessel find is disputed). Beijing is a setting, not the birthplace (the video says "northern" and "a hutong", and the title says "Beijing, China" only as the place).
- **Sensitivities:** Turpan and Astana are shown only as archaeology, a desert oasis on the Silk Road and a dry climate: no politics, no people, no modern events. The tomb is a quiet, respectful paper-cut; the only joke is that the Uncle wants to eat an exhibit (and is stopped). "Spring Festival" is the name used (many countries celebrate the Lunar New Year, said in the description). No Kitchen God, no coin, no north-versus-south ranking. No joke on how anyone speaks; no invented Chinese text (couplets are blank red rectangles); no brand or shop names.
- **Jokes land on the Uncle only** (the weather, the whistle, the cabbage, the chopsticks, the fridge). The frozen ears are a cartoon: cold blue ice, no injury close-up, and he thaws in the same episode.

A food-travel show on the old Silk Road, hosted by a boy and (mostly) interrupted by his uncle. We follow Dad's old dumpling journal, one dumpling per page.
Stop 3: Beijing at Spring Festival, and guotie: crispy potstickers, with a full family recipe below. (Many countries celebrate the Lunar New Year; in Beijing we say Spring Festival.)

Legend says a kind doctor, Zhang Zhongjing, folded ear-shaped dumplings to warm villagers' frozen ears. He was a real physician of the Eastern Han; the dumpling story is folklore.
Silk Road note: real dumplings, over 1,300 years old, were found in dry desert tombs at Astana, near Turpan, on the Silk Road. They are crescent-shaped, about 5 cm long.

Guotie means "pot stick". Beijing is our setting, not the birthplace: jiaozi and guotie are a northern-China tradition.
"Crispy and soft together." – Dad

📝 FULL RECIPE · CRISPY POTSTICKERS (GUOTIE) · about 24 · northern style, ends open

⚠️ GROWN-UP STEP = knife, hot pan, hot oil, splatter, boiling water. Helpers can squeeze, mix, fold and pinch.
🧼 RAW MEAT RULE: wash hands with soap after touching raw meat, never taste the raw filling, keep it in the fridge until you fold, and keep raw filling away from cooked food.
🌾 ALLERGY NOTE: wrappers contain wheat (gluten); the filling has soy and sesame. Check your wrapper and sauce labels.

1. THE CABBAGE (helper job: squeezing)
- 200 g napa cabbage (about 3 leaves) · ½ tsp salt
- ⚠️ GROWN-UP STEP: chop the cabbage very finely with a knife.
- Mix in the salt, wait 10 minutes, then squeeze out the water as hard as you can. Less water, less splatter.

2. THE FILLING (helper job: mixing)
- 250 g ground pork
  · Chicken option: 250 g ground chicken
  · Vegetable option: 200 g firm tofu (pressed dry and crumbled) + 100 g mushrooms, finely chopped by a grown-up
- 2 green onions, finely chopped · 1 tsp grated fresh ginger
- 1 tbsp soy sauce · 1 tsp sesame oil · 1 tsp cornstarch · a pinch of white pepper
- Put everything in a bowl with the squeezed cabbage. Stir in ONE direction with a spoon until sticky, about 1 minute.

3. FOLD (helper job: folding and pinching)
- 24 round dumpling wrappers (store-bought) · a small bowl of water
- Put about 1 tablespoon of filling in a short line across the middle. Don't overfill.
- Wet the edge with a fingertip. Fold the wrapper in half and pinch it closed in the MIDDLE only. Leave both ends a little open: that is the classic northern guotie shape and the easiest fold.
- Prefer them closed? Pinch the whole edge shut and you have jiaozi. Both work.

4. COOK: FRY, STEAM, CRISP (about 12 at a time in a 26 cm / 10-inch non-stick pan with a lid)
- ⚠️ GROWN-UP STEP (hot pan): heat 1 tbsp vegetable oil over medium heat. Line up the guotie close together, flat side down.
- Fry 2-3 minutes, until the bottoms are light golden.
- ⚠️ GROWN-UP STEP (splatter!): pour in 80 ml (⅓ cup) water and cover with the lid RIGHT AWAY. It will hiss and spit. Everyone else, stand back.
- Steam with the lid on for 5-6 minutes, until the water is almost gone.
- Take the lid off. Cook 1-2 more minutes until all the water is gone and the bottoms are deep golden and crispy.
- IS IT COOKED? ⚠️ GROWN-UP STEP: cut one open. The filling should be fully cooked, with no pink and clear juices. With a food thermometer: pork 71 °C / 160 °F, chicken 74 °C / 165 °F. Not there yet? Add a splash of water, lid on, 1-2 minutes more.
- Repeat with the second batch.

OR BOIL THEM (jiaozi style)
- ⚠️ GROWN-UP STEP: bring a big pot of water to a boil. Slide the dumplings in gently with a slotted spoon (no splashing) and stir once so they don't stick.
- Boil about 6-8 minutes. They tend to float when nearly done, but floating is not the test: check one the same way (no pink · pork 71 °C / 160 °F · chicken 74 °C / 165 °F).
- ⚠️ GROWN-UP STEP: lift them out with a slotted spoon.

5. SERVE
- ⚠️ GROWN-UP STEP: put a plate upside down over the pan and flip it, crispy side up.
- Dipping sauce (helpers can mix): 1 tbsp soy sauce + 1 tbsp Chinese black vinegar (or rice vinegar). Optional: a little grated garlic or a few drops of chili oil.
- ⚠️ The inside is very hot. Rest 1 minute before biting.

MAKE-AHEAD TIP: freeze uncooked guotie on a tray so they don't touch, then move them to a bag. Cook from frozen (don't thaw) with a little more water and a few more minutes of steaming, and always check that one is cooked through.
