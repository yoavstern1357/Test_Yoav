## Full recipe (for description)

### Grandma Han's Crispy Family Mandu (gun-mandu, about 30 dumplings)
⚠️ **Grown-up step** = knives, boiling water, steam or a hot pan. Kids: squeezing, mixing, folding, pinching!
🧼 **Raw meat rule:** wash hands with soap after touching raw meat, and never taste the raw filling. Keep the filling in the fridge until you fold.
🌾 **Allergy note:** wrappers contain wheat (gluten); the filling has egg, soy and sesame.

**1. Get the vegetables ready**
- 150 g (about 1½ cups) mung bean sprouts, or finely shredded napa cabbage
- **Grown-up step ⚠️:** blanch the sprouts in boiling water for 1 minute, then drain and rinse with cold water. (Cabbage: sprinkle with ½ tsp salt, wait 15 minutes.)
- **Grown-up step ⚠️ (knife):** chop the sprouts or cabbage finely.
- **Kid job:** put the vegetables in a clean kitchen towel and **squeeze out as much water as you can**.
- 2 green onions, finely chopped **(grown-up step ⚠️, knife)**

**2. Get the tofu ready**
- 200 g firm tofu (about half a block)
- **Kid job:** wrap it in a clean towel and squeeze hard, then crumble it into a big bowl with your hands. *(Uncle: "It's like edible snow!")*

**3. Mix the filling (kid job!)**
Add to the bowl with the tofu and vegetables:
- 200 g ground pork **or** ground chicken
- 1 egg
- 1 tbsp soy sauce
- 1 tsp sesame oil
- 1 clove garlic, finely grated (or ½ tsp garlic powder)
- ½ tsp salt and a pinch of black pepper
- Optional, for grown-up taste buds: ½ cup finely chopped, well-squeezed kimchi, for kimchi mandu like at Seoul's markets (it's spicy!)
- Mix with a spoon or clean hands until everything sticks together. **Wash hands well after touching raw meat.**

🌱 **Vegetarian option:** skip the meat. Instead use 150 g finely chopped mushrooms, cooked in a pan with a little oil until dry **(grown-up step ⚠️, hot pan)** and cooled, plus a small handful of glass noodles (sweet-potato noodles) soaked in hot water until soft and chopped **(grown-up step ⚠️)**. Keep the egg to hold it together, or leave it out for a vegan version (add 1 tbsp cornstarch instead).

**4. Fold (everyone together!)**
- 30 round dumpling wrappers (store-bought), and a small bowl of water
- Put 1 heaped teaspoon of filling in the center. Don't overfill!
- Wet the edge with a fingertip, fold into a **half-moon** and press out the air. Press each one gently onto the tray so it has a **flat bottom** (that's the side that gets crispy).
- Optional round "family" shape (best for steaming or soup): bring the two corners together, dab with water, and pinch them. *(Grandma: "The dumpling gives itself a hug.")*
- Set them on a tray dusted with flour, not touching.
- **Folding line:** one person spoons, one folds, one pinches, one lines them up. Just like Episode 5!

**5. Cook: pan-fried gun-mandu** (all grown-up steps ⚠️, hot pan)
- Kids stand **well back from the stove** for this part: hot oil and steam can spit.
- Heat 1 tbsp oil in a non-stick pan with a lid, over medium heat. Set the mandu in, flat side down, in one layer (cook in batches).
- Fry until the bottoms are golden, about **2 minutes**.
- Add 3 tbsp water and **cover right away** (it spits!). Cook covered for **6–7 minutes**, until the water is gone.
- Take the lid off (**away from your face**) and fry 1 minute more to re-crisp the bottoms.
- **Other ways (also grown-up steps ⚠️):**
  - **Steamed (jjin-mandu):** line a steamer with parchment paper with small holes or cabbage leaves. Steam over boiling water for **10–12 minutes**. Open the lid **away from your face**: the steam can burn.
  - **Boiled (mul-mandu):** drop into a big pot of gently boiling water. When they float, cook **2–3 minutes more** (about 6–8 minutes in total).
- **Is it done?** Cut one open: the filling should be hot all the way through with no pink. With a food thermometer: **71 °C / 160 °F for pork**, **74 °C / 165 °F for chicken**. If not, cook 2–3 minutes more.
- Let them cool for a couple of minutes before little ones take a bite: the filling stays hot.

**6. Dipping sauce (kid job!)**
- 2 tbsp soy sauce + 1 tbsp rice vinegar + a pinch of sesame seeds. (Grown-ups can add a pinch of red pepper flakes.)

**7. Bonus: Chuncheon New Year soup (tteok-mandu-guk, from Episode 5)** (grown-up steps ⚠️)
- Soak 300 g sliced rice cakes (tteok) in cold water for 20 minutes if they are hard.
- Bring 1.5 liters of broth (beef, chicken, anchovy or vegetable) to a boil. Season with 1 tbsp soy sauce.
- Add the rice cakes and 8–10 uncooked mandu. Cook until the mandu float and the rice cakes are soft, then 2–3 minutes more.
- Optional: drizzle in 1 beaten egg while stirring, until set. Top with green onion and roasted seaweed strips.
- *(Uncle: "One bowl, one year older… I'll just have half.")*

**Freezing tip:** freeze extra uncooked mandu on a floured tray, then move them into a freezer bag. Cook from frozen, adding 2–3 minutes (and check doneness as above). For pan-frying from frozen, don't thaw: use the same steps, with a little more water under the lid.

## Notes (recipe accuracy and safety)
- Tofu, mung bean sprouts (or cabbage), green onion and pork are a classic home mandu filling; squeezing the water out is the key step in Korean home recipes (wet filling tears the wrappers).
- **Pan-fry method:** fry the bottoms golden, add a little water, cover to steam the filling through, then uncover to re-crisp. This is the standard home "potsticker" method for gun-mandu. Times are for fresh, thin, store-bought wrappers; thicker or frozen mandu need longer, so the doneness check is always shown.
- Steaming times in recipe sources range from about 5 to 12 minutes, so the options use the longer end (10–12) plus a doneness check.
- Safe internal temperatures: ground pork 160 °F (71 °C), poultry 165 °F (74 °C), per USDA.
- **Hot-pan safety:** every pan step carries the `⚠️ GROWN-UP STEP · hot pan` badge; the kids are shown standing back from the stove, and the Uncle's sizzle gag happens *away* from the pan (he jumps into Jun's arms from the safe spot), so nobody models leaning over hot oil.
- Kimchi is optional and marked as spicy; there is no raw-meat tasting anywhere in the script (the Uncle only "tastes" plain tofu, and the gag of eating raw mandu is stopped by the kids).
- **Montage rule:** every on-screen step that involves a knife, boiling water, steam or a hot pan carries the `⚠️ GROWN-UP STEP` badge; every quantity shown on screen matches the full recipe.
- **Name-game fact card:** jjin-mandu from jjida (to steam), mul-mandu from mul (water), gun-mandu from gupda (to grill/roast; in practice pan-fried). Sources describe gun-mandu slightly differently (grilled vs. pan-fried), so the card says "pan-fried/grilled".
- **Busan Chinatown (new):** the area on Shanghai Street in Choryang-dong (Dong District), directly across from Busan Station near the port. Its story is usually traced to 1884, when the Qing (Chinese) government set up a consulate and concession area near the port, and Chinese families (Hwagyo, Korean-Chinese) settled around it. It was designated a Chinatown Special Zone in 2007, and the big gate at the entrance symbolizes the friendship between Busan and Shanghai. Its dumpling shops (some run by Hwagyo families for generations) are known for gun-mandu and often have queues. Because sources date the very first settlement slightly differently (some mention an earlier concession framework in the late 1870s), the card says "**from the 1880s**", not an exact year. Don't name or draw any real shop.
- **Busan → Japan ferry:** ferries from Busan's international passenger terminal sail to Japan (Fukuoka, Shimonoseki, and an overnight ferry to Osaka). There is **no direct Busan–Kobe ferry**; Kobe sits on the same bay, a short train ride from Osaka. The map line is stylized and ends at Kobe; Episode 7's opening can show the overnight ferry arriving in the bay and a quick train hop to Kobe. Don't name a ferry company.
- **Story continuity:** Dad's handwritten card pays off Episode 5's cliffhanger ("what your father left"). The frozen mandu "for the road… to share" is the arc's **sharing** clue (see `production/season-map.md`). The fluffy steamed bun on the torn page teases Episode 7 (Kobe's butaman, clue "warmth").
- **Characters:** the Han family is fictional (Grandma Han from Chuncheon; her son's family lives in Busan, in Choryang). All jokes are on the Uncle.
- **Dialogue pace:** all sections stay at or under 3 words per second (e.g. the recap beat is about 20 words in 14 s; the ending is about 30 words in 22 s).
