# Episode 04: "Uncle Fights a Frying Pan" (Japan arc, part 1 of 3: Arrival)

**Length:** about 60s Short · **Location:** a small lantern-lit gyoza counter in a Tokyo side street, Japan · **Dumpling:** Gyoza

## Script

**[0–3s] HOOK.** *SIZZLE!* Close-up: UNCLE holds a frying-pan lid like a knight's shield. Little oil drops pop behind it.
> **UNCLE:** It's ATTACKING me!

**[3–11s] THE JOURNEY.** Rewind-style swoosh. The BOY opens Dad's leather **journal**. On the hand-drawn map, a dotted line glows from **Shanghai** across the sea to **Tokyo**. A tiny cartoon plane flies along it. Inside the plane, UNCLE hugs the gift from the Shanghai GRANDMA: a little bamboo steamer. He opens the lid. It's empty. Crumbs on his mustache.
> **BOY (V.O.):** Page two… Tokyo.
> **BOY:** Uncle, that was for later!
> **UNCLE:** It *is* later.

**[11–14s] TITLE CARD.** `🥟 #04 GYOZA · Tokyo, Japan`

**[14–29s] THE GAG.** At the counter, kind CHEF TAKA (60s, white headband, calm smile) slides over a plate of gyoza: golden-brown bottoms, soft pleated tops. UNCLE stands up.
> **UNCLE:** I can do that. For science!

UNCLE grabs a pan of gyoza and flips it like a TV chef. *FWOOMP!* The gyoza fly up… and stick to the ceiling. Silence. One gyoza slowly peels off and drops right into his open mouth.
> **UNCLE:** *(chewing, proud)* …Chef's kiss.
> **CHEF TAKA:** *(gently)* Next time… we don't flip.

**[29–44s] "LEGEND SAYS…" / HISTORY.** Storybook-style animation (paper-cut look, warm colors). A Chinese jiaozi waves hello to a Japanese gyoza across a map.
> **BOY (V.O.):** Gyoza comes from Chinese jiaozi. Chinese food reached Japan's port towns over a century ago.
> **BOY (V.O.):** Many people say gyoza truly took off after 1945, when families came home from northeast China, missing those dumplings.

Quick map pop-ups: `Utsunomiya 🥟` and `Hamamatsu 🥟`, two little cities waving gyoza flags at each other.
> **BOY (V.O.):** Today, two cities both proudly say: "We're the gyoza city!"

On-screen fact card: `Gyoza = Japan's name for jiaozi 🥟 · In Japan, usually pan-fried`

**[44–53s] DAD'S WISDOM.** Soft music. Sepia memory: DAD's silhouette (conical hat, from behind) at a little stove, steam rising.
> **BOY:** Dad wrote: *"Be like a gyoza. A little crispy outside, soft on top, and full of good stuff inside."*

**[53–60s] BUTTON.** UNCLE stares up. One gyoza is *still* stuck to the ceiling. He stands on a stool, chopsticks stretched as far as they go.
> **UNCLE:** We are NOT leaving that one behind.
> **BOY:** *(to camera)* Dad was right… Next: how Tokyo eats gyoza! 👇

## Sources (history beat)
- Kikkoman Institute for International Food Culture, "Gyoza" (FOOD CULTURE no. 27): Chinese dishes incl. jiaozi arrived via Yokohama/Kobe Chinatowns in the Meiji era; frequent eating came later, spread by people returning from China: https://www.kikkoman.com/jp/c/kiifc/foodculture/pdf_27/e_012_013.pdf
- The Japan Times feature on gyoza: repatriation from mainland China sparked a gyoza boom; boiled jiaozi is common in China, pan-fried gyoza became the norm in Japan: https://features.japantimes.co.jp/gyoza
- nippon.com, "The Japanese Fixation on Rice": gyoza became a regular household dish after WWII, when many Japanese were repatriated from China: https://www.nippon.com/en/column/g00589/
- Utsunomiya: the army's 14th Division was based there and was stationed in Manchuria; this is the commonly given reason for the city's gyoza culture: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/14th_Division_(Imperial_Japanese_Army) and ANA Japan Travel Planner (Tochigi): https://www.ana.co.jp/en/us/japan-travel-planner/tochigi/0000002.html
- Utsunomiya vs. Hamamatsu "gyoza capital" rivalry (government household spending survey): The Japan Times (2012): https://www.japantimes.co.jp/news/2012/02/02/national/hamamatsu-new-gyoza-capital/ · nippon.com (Miyazaki tops the ranking in 2021): https://nippon.com/en/japan-topics/g02104 · InsideJapan Tours news: https://www.insidejapantours.com/japan-news/3892/hamamatsu-takes-gyoza-dumpling-crown-again/

## Accuracy notes
- "Many people say" is used for the 1945 boom because the detailed story (which soldiers or settlers, which first shops) comes mostly from local and tourism accounts. The broad point (postwar returnees from China made gyoza popular) appears in several reliable sources.
- Some sources tell an Edo-period legend of a scholar serving dumplings to a lord; it's left out because it's a folk story with no firm record.
- The spending ranking changes every year (Utsunomiya, Hamamatsu and recently Miyazaki have all been first), so the script only says both cities "proudly say" they're the gyoza city. Don't put a year's winner on screen.
- Keep the WWII mention soft and short: "families came home". No war imagery in the storybook animation.
