## Full recipe (for description)
Paste this whole section into the YouTube description, under the intro text.

### Easy Open-Top Shūmai (about 20 shūmai, no steamer needed)
⚠️ **Grown-up step** = knives, the hot pan, steam and the hot lid. Kids: mixing, folding, squeezing, topping!
🧼 **Raw meat rule:** wash hands with soap after touching raw meat or shrimp, and never taste the raw filling. Keep raw filling away from cooked food.
🌾 **Allergy note:** wrappers contain wheat (gluten); the filling has soy and sesame; shrimp is a shellfish. Check your wrapper packet (some contain egg).

**1. The filling (kid job: mixing!)**
- ½ onion (about 100 g), very finely chopped **(⚠️ grown-up step, knife)**
- 2 tbsp potato starch or cornstarch
- **Kids:** toss the onion with the starch in a big bowl first. *(Auntie Lin: "Starch hugs the onion juice.")* It keeps the filling juicy and stops the wrappers going soggy.
- 250 g ground pork
  - **Chicken option:** 250 g ground chicken
  - **Shrimp option:** 200 g raw peeled shrimp, chopped by a grown-up **(⚠️ knife)**, or half shrimp + half pork
  - **Veggie option:** 200 g firm tofu (pressed dry and crumbled) + 100 g mushrooms, finely chopped by a grown-up **(⚠️ knife)**
- 1 tsp grated fresh ginger · 1 tbsp soy sauce · 1 tsp sesame oil · 1 tsp sugar · ¼ tsp salt · a pinch of white pepper
- Add everything to the onion. Mix with clean hands or a spoon until sticky, about 1 minute. **Wash hands after touching raw meat.**

**2. The open-top fold (kid job: no pleats!)**
- 20 square wonton wrappers (or round/square shūmai wrappers) · 20 frozen green peas or small cubes of carrot
- Make an **"OK" circle** with your thumb and pointer finger. Lay a wrapper on top.
- Put 1 heaped teaspoon of filling in the middle and gently push it down into the circle. The wrapper folds up around it like a little cup, open at the top.
- Squeeze gently to shape a "waist", smooth the top with a wet spoon, and tap the bottom on the table so it stands flat.
- Press one pea (or carrot cube) on top. *(Uncle's has 12 peas. It's the mayor.)*

**3. Steam in a frying pan** (no steamer needed; a 26 cm / 10-inch pan with a lid)
- Line the cold pan with 3–4 cabbage (or napa cabbage) leaves. Line up the shūmai on top, not touching.
- **⚠️ Grown-up step (hot pan + steam):** pour **100 ml (about ⅓ cup + 1 tbsp) water** around the edge, put the lid on and heat over medium.
- When the water bubbles, steam for **10–12 minutes**, lid on. If the pan goes dry, a grown-up adds 2–3 tbsp water.
- **⚠️ Grown-up step (hot lid):** lift the lid **away from your face**: the steam can burn.
- Using a bamboo or metal steamer instead? Line it with cabbage leaves or holey parchment and steam over boiling water for **10–12 minutes**.

**4. Is it cooked?** ⚠️ **Grown-up step:** cut one open. The filling should be firm and hot all the way through, with no pink and clear juices. With a food thermometer: **pork 71°C / 160°F**, **chicken 74°C / 165°F**. **Shrimp:** pink and opaque all the way through (if mixed with pork, go by the pork temperature). Not done? Steam 2–3 minutes more.

**5. Serve**
- Dip in soy sauce. Grown-ups can add a tiny dab of Japanese mustard (karashi), it's spicy!
- Eat the steamed cabbage too.
- ⚠️ Careful: the inside is very hot. Let them rest a minute before little ones bite.

**Make-ahead tip:** freeze uncooked shūmai on a tray so they don't touch, then move to a bag. Steam from frozen (don't thaw), adding about 5 more minutes, and check they're cooked through as above.

## Notes (accuracy and safety)
- **No brand names:** the 1928 shūmai maker is a well-known Yokohama company; it's described only as "a station shop". Don't show its name, its gourd-shaped soy-sauce bottles or its packaging. Its own spelling "shiumai" is not used on screen; the episode uses the common Japanese "shūmai".
- **"Many people in Yokohama still buy…"** is softened from sales figures (sources give 30,000–50,000+ boxes a day, and they disagree), so no number goes on screen.
- **"Largest Chinatown in Japan"** is well supported. Some travel sites say "largest in Asia"; that's not on screen.
- **Canton link:** many early Yokohama Chinese came from Guangdong (Canton). This quietly links back to Ep 2 (Guangzhou) and the Cantonese name siu mai. Say "many", not "all".
- **Shrimp doneness:** FoodSafety.gov gives a visual test for shrimp (pearly or white, and opaque), not a number, so the badge says "pink & opaque all through". If shrimp and pork are mixed, go by the pork temperature.
- **Frying-pan steaming:** recipe sources give 8–10 minutes for pan-steamed dumplings; the script uses 10–12 minutes because the filling is a solid little cup of raw meat, plus a doneness check. Thicker or frozen shūmai need longer.
- **Kids' safety:** kids never touch the pan once it's on the heat, never lift the lid, and never taste raw filling (the Uncle's attempt is stopped). Karashi mustard is "grown-ups only" and marked spicy.
- **Auntie Lin** is fictional (a Yokohama-born Chinatown cook). No shop name or real signage on screen.
- **Dad's line and clues:** the stamp line ties the three Japan clues together (Warmth, Ep 7 · Friendly rivals, Ep 8 · Keep it simple, Ep 9).
- **Next country:** Ep 10 is Vigan, Ilocos Sur (Philippines), with its deep-fried orange empanada. The doodle (orange half-moon + two flags shaking hands) teases Ep 10's clue, "Mix (two worlds in one bite)". The sea route south fits the Maritime Silk Road season.
