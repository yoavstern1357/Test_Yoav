# Episode 02: "The Grandma Who Folds Faster Than Light" (China arc, part 2 of 3: Culture)

**Length:** about 2:00 (1:50–2:10) · vertical 9:16 · YouTube Short · **Location:** a busy morning street in Shanghai · **Dumpling:** Xiaolongbao

## Script

**[0:00–0:04] HOOK.** Early morning. A tiny GRANDMA at a dumpling stall folds dumplings so fast her hands are a blur. *Whoosh whoosh whoosh.*
> **UNCLE:** *(jaw drops)* She's… a superhero.

**[0:04–0:08] TITLE CARD.** `🥟 #02 HOW SHANGHAI EATS XIAOLONGBAO`

**[0:08–0:20] MORNING STREET (sights and sounds).** The city wakes up. Bicycle bells, sizzling pans, steamer lids clattering, a radio playing softly. A friendly line of people waits at the stall: a girl in a school uniform, an office worker, an old man with a birdcage. Steam rises from stall after stall in the golden light.
> **BOY (V.O.):** Shanghai mornings smell like steam… and breakfast.
> **UNCLE:** *(sniffing in every direction)* Which way? ALL the ways!

**[0:20–0:34] CULTURE MOMENT.** The BOY and UNCLE sit at a small table on the street. People on bikes, steam from many stalls.
> **BOY (V.O.):** In Shanghai, lots of people eat soup dumplings for breakfast, hot from the steamer, with black vinegar and thin strips of ginger.

GRANDMA holds up one dumpling and counts the folds with a smile. On-screen: tiny folds light up one by one.
> **GRANDMA:** Many folds = a careful cook. Some famous shops aim for 18!

**[0:34–0:44] FUN FACT CARD: THE "FOUR HEAVENLY KINGS".** A neighbor's tray slides past with four classic breakfast foods. Each one pops up with a little label.
On-screen: `FUN FACT 🥢 Shanghai's classic breakfast "Four Heavenly Kings": dabing (flatbread) · youtiao (fried dough stick) · cifantuan (sticky rice roll) · doujiang (soy milk)`
> **BOY (V.O.):** Shanghai even has breakfast "kings". Four of them!
> **UNCLE:** *(tucking in a napkin)* I bow to all four.

**[0:44–0:52] THE GAG, PART 1: VINEGAR.** UNCLE dunks his dumpling into the vinegar dish… and keeps dunking, like a tea bag. He takes a big sip of vinegar by mistake. His whole face puckers into a tiny raisin. His mustache curls up.
> **UNCLE:** *(squeaky)* Sour… science.
> **GRANDMA:** *(giggling)* Just a little dip!

**[0:52–1:06] THE GAG, PART 2: FOLDING.** UNCLE tries to fold one himself. He makes a lumpy, giant "dumpling" the size of his head. Grandma pats his shoulder kindly.
> **GRANDMA:** Good start. *(gives him a tiny perfect one instead)*
> **UNCLE:** *(eats it slowly, very proud of himself)* Patience!

**[1:06–1:16] FUN FACT CARD: A PROTECTED TRADITION.** GRANDMA's hands, slow-motion now, pleat after pleat. A soft gold border appears around the frame.
On-screen: `FUN FACT 🏮 The Nanxiang way of making xiaolongbao is on China's national list of intangible cultural heritage (since 2014).`
> **GRANDMA:** These hands learned from my teacher. Hers learned from hers.

**[1:16–1:30] THE STORY MOVES.** GRANDMA notices the BOY's conical hat and freezes. She takes an old photo from her apron: a tall man in the **same hat**, smiling at her stall, years ago.
> **GRANDMA:** I know this hat. He came every morning. He said: "the soup is the secret."
> **BOY:** *(whispers)* Dad…

The BOY opens the journal: under Shanghai, a faded line appears: `Secret #1: patience.` ✨

**[1:30–1:44] HEART MOMENT.** Quiet music. The street noise fades. The BOY holds the photo with both hands. UNCLE, for once, isn't eating. He lowers his chopsticks and puts a big, gentle hand on the BOY's shoulder. GRANDMA slides a warm cup of soy milk toward the BOY.
> **UNCLE:** *(softly)* He sat right here, kiddo.
> **BOY:** *(eyes closed, smiling)* Then so will we.

**[1:44–2:02] BUTTON + CLIFFHANGER.**
> **GRANDMA:** Come back tomorrow. I'll teach you to make them.
> **UNCLE:** *(already tying an apron)* We're not leaving.
GRANDMA leans close to the BOY and whispers.
> **GRANDMA:** …the same way I taught your father.
The BOY's eyes go wide. Dad learned *here*?
> **BOY:** *(to camera)* Next episode: we make soup dumplings at home! 👇

## Notes
- "Some famous shops aim for 18 folds" is well known (often linked to a famous dumpling restaurant chain). Keep it as "some shops", and don't name a brand.
- Breakfast with vinegar and ginger: a common, well-documented way to eat them in Shanghai.
- The vinegar gag is about *too much*, not about the vinegar: keep Grandma's "just a little dip" so the custom itself looks good.
- Grandma's "I taught your father" sets up Episode 3. Episode 3 ends with her gift (the little bamboo steamer used in Episode 4).
