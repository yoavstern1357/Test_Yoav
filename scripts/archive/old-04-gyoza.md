# Episode 04: "Uncle Fights a Frying Pan" (Japan arc, part 1 of 3: Arrival)

**Length:** about 2:00 Short (1:50–2:10), vertical 9:16 · **Location:** Tokyo, Japan: a train platform, a busy crossing, then a small lantern-lit gyoza counter in a side street · **Dumpling:** Gyoza

## Script

**[0:00–0:03] HOOK.** *SIZZLE!* Close-up: UNCLE holds a frying-pan lid like a knight's shield. Little oil drops pop behind it.
> **UNCLE:** It's ATTACKING me!

**[0:03–0:06] REWIND.** Freeze frame. A record-scratch, then a rewind swoosh.
> **BOY (V.O.):** Okay. Let's go back.

**[0:06–0:20] THE JOURNEY.** The BOY opens Dad's leather **journal**. On the hand-drawn map, a dotted line glows from **Shanghai** across the sea to **Tokyo**. A tiny cartoon plane flies along it. Inside the plane, UNCLE hugs the gift from the Shanghai GRANDMA: a little bamboo steamer. He opens the lid. It's empty. Crumbs on his mustache.
> **BOY (V.O.):** Page two… Tokyo.
> **BOY:** Uncle, that was for later!
> **UNCLE:** It *is* later.

UNCLE peeks into the empty steamer again, hopeful, as if more might appear. It doesn't.
> **UNCLE:** *(whispering to the steamer)* …I'm sorry.

**[0:20–0:23] TITLE CARD.** `🥟 #04 GYOZA · Tokyo, Japan`

**[0:23–0:45] ARRIVING IN TOKYO.** Quick, bright postcard shots with real sounds:
- A clean train platform. A short, cheerful tune plays as the train gets ready to leave. UNCLE hums along, loudly and wrong. The BOY gently pulls him into a line of people waiting politely.
- A huge crossing: the lights turn green and crowds walk in every direction at once, like a slow dance. UNCLE stops in the middle, sniffing the air like a cartoon dog. The BOY guides him across by the sleeve.
- Dusk. A narrow side street of small shops with glowing red paper lanterns. A soft *sizzle* from a doorway with a short cloth curtain.

On-screen fact card (during the platform shot): `🎶 Many train stations in Tokyo play their own short tune when a train is about to leave!`
> **BOY (V.O.):** So many stations have their own song.
> **UNCLE:** *(humming)* Mine's called "Lunch."
> **BOY (V.O.):** And then… that sound.

**[0:45–1:08] THE GAG.** At the counter, kind CHEF TAKA (60s, white headband, calm smile) slides over a plate of gyoza: golden-brown bottoms, soft pleated tops. UNCLE stands up.
> **UNCLE:** I can do that. For science!

UNCLE grabs a pan of gyoza and flips it like a TV chef. *FWOOMP!* The gyoza fly up… and stick to the ceiling. Silence. One gyoza slowly peels off and drops right into his open mouth.
> **UNCLE:** *(chewing, proud)* …Chef's kiss.
> **CHEF TAKA:** *(gently)* Next time… we don't flip.

Escalation: UNCLE tries a "small, gentle flip" of one gyoza with his chopsticks. It spins up… and lands on his bald head like a tiny hat. He freezes, eyes up.
> **BOY:** *(trying not to laugh)* It suits you.
> **UNCLE:** *(dignified)* I meant that.

**[1:08–1:30] "LEGEND SAYS…" / HISTORY.** Storybook-style animation (paper-cut look, warm colors). A Chinese jiaozi waves hello to a Japanese gyoza across a map.
> **BOY (V.O.):** Gyoza comes from Chinese jiaozi. Chinese food reached Japan's port towns over a century ago.
> **BOY (V.O.):** Many people say gyoza truly took off after 1945, when families came home from northeast China, missing those dumplings.

Quick map pop-ups: `Utsunomiya 🥟` and `Hamamatsu 🥟`, two little cities waving gyoza flags at each other.
> **BOY (V.O.):** Today, two cities both proudly say: "We're the gyoza city!"

On-screen fact card: `Gyoza = Japan's name for jiaozi 🥟 · In Japan, usually pan-fried`

Second fact card, with a storybook drawing of the statue: `🗿 Outside Utsunomiya Station stands a stone statue of a goddess wrapped in a gyoza!` UNCLE (in a small corner bubble) bows to it very seriously.
> **UNCLE:** A hero.

**[1:30–1:50] HEART MOMENT + DAD'S WISDOM.** Later. The street is quiet; only the lanterns hum. CHEF TAKA has made a fresh plate. One gyoza is left. UNCLE's chopsticks reach for it… stop… and slide the plate to the BOY.
> **BOY:** You saved one for me?
> **UNCLE:** *(softly)* Your dad always did.

The BOY smiles with his eyes closed. Soft music. Sepia memory: DAD's silhouette (conical hat, from behind) at a little stove, steam rising.
> **BOY:** Dad wrote: *"Be like a gyoza. A little crispy outside, soft on top, and full of good stuff inside."*

**[1:50–2:00] BUTTON + CLIFFHANGER.** UNCLE stands on a stool, chopsticks stretched toward the one gyoza *still* stuck to the ceiling.
> **UNCLE:** We are NOT leaving that one behind.

Behind them, CHEF TAKA stops wiping the counter. He stares at the BOY's conical hat.
> **CHEF TAKA:** *(quietly)* That hat… I've seen it before.
> **BOY:** *(turns, eyes wide; to camera)* …Next time! 👇

## Accuracy notes
- "Many people say" is used for the 1945 boom because the detailed story (which soldiers or settlers, which first shops) comes mostly from local and tourism accounts. The broad point (postwar returnees from China made gyoza popular) appears in several reliable sources.
- Some sources tell an Edo-period legend of a scholar serving dumplings to a lord; it's left out because it's a folk story with no firm record.
- The spending ranking changes every year (Utsunomiya, Hamamatsu and recently Miyazaki have all been first), so the script only says both cities "proudly say" they're the gyoza city. Don't put a year's winner on screen.
- Keep the WWII mention soft and short: "families came home". No war imagery in the storybook animation.
- Departure tunes: the card says "many" stations, not "every" station, because some stations use plain chimes and tunes change over time. Don't name or play a real copyrighted melody; use an original short jingle.
- Scramble crossing: shown as Tokyo texture only. Crowd numbers vary a lot between sources (official tourism sites say 1,000+ per light), so no figure on screen. Don't show real shop signs or ad screens.
- The Venus statue is shown in storybook style, not as a photo-real copy. Keep the "bow" gag on the Uncle's enthusiasm, never mocking the statue.
