## How to read the tables
- **Method:** `I2V` = image-to-video with FIRST + LAST frame conditioning (START and END keyframes), 3-5 s, one clear action, continuous motion with facial expression changes. `CODE` = animated paper-cut / map layers built locally (still continuous motion, never a held still). `I2V+CODE` = I2V with a code insert. Camera moves, speed lines, sweat drops, impact frames and fact cards are added in the edit.
- **Keyframes are both orientations:** every START and END is generated twice (9:16 and 16:9), same seed and references, composition re-framed, not cropped.
- **START / END:** describe the frame, not the story. Where an END is the next clip's START the pair is a *match* (chain: C06 to C07, C18 to C19 (ECU), C20 to C21 (hand-off of the ice pack scene), C32 to C33 (dissolve)). Generate the END first and reuse it as the next START.
- **Caption zone:** the free band for captions in that clip. TOP = upper band (9:16: y 180-430 of 1920; 16:9: y 60-190 of 1080). BOTTOM = lower band (9:16: y 1420-1700; 16:9: y 870-1010). Captions follow the voice, in the approved style, and never sit on a face, a hand, the dumpling or key action. The title cards and fact cards use the opposite band from the captions.
- Voice: `SPEAKER [delivery note]: “words”`. Brackets are never spoken and never counted. `...` is a short thinking pause. `(V.O.)` is off-screen narration over the picture; `(to lens)` is a look at camera. When two speakers share a clip, the second starts a hair early on the last word of the first (at most 0.3 s overlap, record every voice clean and overlap in the edit).
- The Narrator speaks only with a **gong plus a 0.4 s paper-scroll edge sliding across the frame edge** (a CODE overlay that does not touch faces); the full scroll unrolls only for the legend.

Stop 1: Nanxiang, an old canal town now part of Shanghai, and the soup dumpling (xiaolongbao).

How does soup get inside a dumpling? It goes in as jelly, made from pork-skin stock that is boiled for hours and chilled until it sets. The cook chops the jelly into the meat, and the steam melts it back into soup. That is also why these dumplings are steamed, never boiled: the thin skin would burst.
Silk Road note: the Silk Roads were a network of land and sea routes. Near Shanghai, the old port of Qinglong shipped ceramics to Japan and Korea more than a thousand years ago.

How many folds? Cooks say 14, 16 or 18. Ask three cooks, get three answers.
Legend says that around 1871 a shop in Nanxiang sold one very big meat bun, then made it smaller and added soup set into jelly. Other towns claim this dumpling too, so we say: legend.

How to eat one safely (grown-ups help with hot soup): lift it by the top knot, put it on a spoon, make a tiny hole, wait, then sip the soup first. Then eat the dumpling with black vinegar and ginger. The soup inside is very hot.
