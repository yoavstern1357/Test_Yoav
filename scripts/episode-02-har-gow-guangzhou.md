**[0:33–0:50] THE GAG, PART 1: TOO-STRONG CHOPSTICKS.** AUNTIE LAN, the teahouse's dim sum cook (fictional, flour on her apron, kind laugh lines), sets down a steamer of four har gow. They wobble, shiny and pink.
> **BOY:** Uncle, gentle hands! The skin is thin…
> **UNCLE:** For science! *(grabs one and squeezes HARD)*
*SQUISH. POING!* The har gow shoots out of his chopsticks like a wet bar of soap, bounces off the teapot (*tink!*), spins in the air… and lands in UNCLE's own teacup. *PLOP.* Tea splashes his face. His mustache drips.
> **UNCLE:** *(dripping, tiny voice)* …Shrimp tea?

**[0:50–1:04] THE GAG, PART 2: TEA MANNERS.** AUNTIE LAN calmly fishes out the dumpling and pours UNCLE fresh tea. His mouth is full, so he tries to say thanks anyway.
> **UNCLE:** *(muffled)* Thnmk mmph!
AUNTIE LAN smiles and taps two bent fingers on the table. *Tap tap.*
> **BOY (V.O.):** Two-finger tap… means "thank you."
UNCLE copies her, but much too hard. *BANG BANG!* Every teacup on the table jumps in the air and lands again. The BOY catches one.
On-screen: `FUN FACT 🫖 At yum cha, a teapot lid left open means "more hot water, please!"`
UNCLE, still hungry, lifts the lid of the empty dumpling **steamer**, and stares hopefully at the waiter.
> **AUNTIE LAN:** *(laughing)* That's only for tea.

**[1:30–1:42] THE STORY MOVES.** AUNTIE LAN notices the BOY's conical hat. She goes quiet, then smiles.

She takes the BOY's hands and shows him: soft fingers, light pinches. The BOY opens the journal. Under Guangzhou, in Dad's faded pen, a line glows: *"Gentle hands. Let it show what's inside."* ✨

**[1:42–1:54] HEART MOMENT.** Quiet music; the teahouse noise fades. The BOY folds one har gow, slowly, with AUNTIE LAN guiding his fingers. It holds. Through the skin, the pink shrimp shows. UNCLE lifts it with his chopsticks, both hands shaking, barely touching it.
> **UNCLE:** *(whispering)* Gentle… gentle…
He sets it down on the BOY's plate without a single tear.
> **UNCLE:** *(softly)* You first, kiddo.

## Notes
- **Wording:** the origin is folklore with competing versions (village and teahouse names and dates differ between sources), so the script says "Many people believe…", names no teahouse, and says "a village near Guangzhou, early 1900s" only in the storyboard art, not in dialogue.
- **Respect:** the jokes are on the Uncle (too-strong chopsticks, too-hard tapping, the steamer lid). The customs themselves (the tap, the open lid) are shown working well, done kindly by Auntie Lan.
- **Finger tap:** many explain it as a tiny "bow" of thanks, with a story about a Qing emperor travelling in disguise. That story is unproven, so it's left out of the script.
- **Auntie Lan** is fictional. No real teahouse, brand or chef is shown or named.
- **"Sea road" wording** matches the season map (Season 1 = Maritime Silk Road). Some sources call Quanzhou the main port in later centuries (Song–Yuan), so the card says "an early start", not "the start".
- **Continuity:** Episode 1's Grandma sent them south ("the dim sum city"). Dad's clue here is **gentle hands** (season map). The red paper-cut and "crispy and soft" set up Episode 3 (Beijing, guotie, clue: balance). Shanghai gets only a check mark here; the 🇨🇳 stamp happens once, at the end of Episode 3.
- **Images needed:** hook (eye through the har gow), boat down the coast, Pearl River dawn + Canton Tower, old trading-ship "ghost" overlay, teahouse wide shot, flying har gow + shrimp tea, teacups jumping, storybook riverside teahouse, pleat counter, Auntie Lan guiding the Boy's hands, Uncle's gentle chopsticks, red paper-cut + train to Beijing.
