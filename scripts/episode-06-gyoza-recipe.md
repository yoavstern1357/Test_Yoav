## Recipe: Crispy Pan-Fried Gyoza (about 24 gyoza)
⚠️ **Grown-up step** = knives, the hot pan, hot oil and splatter. Kids: squeezing, mixing, folding, pleating!
🧼 Wash hands after touching raw meat, and keep raw filling away from cooked food.

**1. The cabbage (kid job: squeezing!)**
- 200 g napa cabbage (about 3 leaves)
- ½ tsp salt
- ⚠️ **Grown-up step:** chop the cabbage very finely with a knife.
- Kids: mix in the salt and wait 10 minutes. Then squeeze out the water with your hands, as hard as you can! *(Chef Taka: "Less water, less splatter.")*

**2. The filling (kid job: mixing!)**
- 250 g ground pork
  - **Chicken option:** 250 g ground chicken
  - **Vegetable option:** 200 g firm tofu (pressed dry and crumbled) + 100 g mushrooms, finely chopped by a grown-up
- 2 green onions, finely chopped · 1 small garlic clove, grated · 1 tsp grated fresh ginger
- 1 tbsp soy sauce · 1 tsp sesame oil · 1 tsp potato starch or cornstarch
- a pinch of sugar · a pinch of white pepper
- Put everything in a bowl with the squeezed cabbage. Mix with clean hands or a spoon until sticky, about 1 minute.

**3. Fold (kid job: folding and pleating!)**
- 24 round gyoza wrappers (store-bought) · a small bowl of water
- Put 1 heaped teaspoon of filling in the middle. Don't overfill.
- Wet half the edge with a fingertip. Fold the wrapper in half like a taco.
- Make 4–6 little pleats along the front edge, pressing each one onto the flat back edge. Pinch the whole edge closed.
- Stand each gyoza up on a plate so it has a flat bottom. *(Uncle: "They're sitting! Like little students!")*

**4. Cook: fry, steam, crisp** (one batch of about 12 in a 26 cm / 10-inch non-stick pan with a lid)
- ⚠️ **Grown-up step (hot pan):** heat 1 tbsp vegetable oil over medium heat. Line up the gyoza flat side down, close together.
- Fry **2–3 minutes**, until the bottoms are light golden.
- ⚠️ **Grown-up step (splatter!):** pour in **80 ml (⅓ cup) water** and cover with the lid *right away*. It will hiss and spit. Kids stand back.
- Steam with the lid on for **5–6 minutes**, until the water is almost gone and the wrappers look a little see-through.
- Take the lid off. Cook **1–2 more minutes** until all the water is gone and the bottoms are deep golden and crispy. Drizzle ½ tsp sesame oil around the edge if you like.
- **Is it cooked?** ⚠️ **Grown-up step:** cut one open. The filling should be fully cooked with no pink, and the juices clear. With a food thermometer: **pork 71°C / 160°F**, **chicken 74°C / 165°F**.
- Repeat with the second batch.

**✨ Optional: crispy "wings" (hanetsuki)**
- In step 4, use this instead of plain water: **80 ml water + 1 tsp flour (or potato starch)**, stirred well.
- When the water cooks off, it leaves a thin, lacy, crispy sheet joining all the gyoza together. *(Boy: "Gyoza… holding hands!")*

**5. Serve the Tokyo way**
- ⚠️ **Grown-up step:** put a plate upside down over the pan and turn the whole thing over, so the crispy side faces up. *(Callback: "We don't flip the gyoza… we flip the PLATE.")*
- Dipping sauce (kids can mix): 1 tbsp soy sauce + 1 tbsp rice vinegar + a few drops of chili oil (rayu), optional.
- Eat with a bowl of rice, miso soup, or next to a bowl of ramen.
- ⚠️ Careful: the inside is very hot. Let them rest a minute before biting.

**Make-ahead tip:** freeze uncooked gyoza on a tray so they don't touch, then move to a bag. Cook from frozen (don't thaw), adding a few more minutes of steaming, and check they're cooked through.

## Notes
- Times in the recipe are for thin store-bought wrappers and a medium flame. Thicker wrappers or a crowded pan need 1–2 more minutes of steaming; always check one is cooked through.
- Chef Taka's kitchen and the gift pan carry no brand names or logos.
- Dad's clue ("balance") is shown, not explained: the fry-steam-crisp method is literally crispy and soft in one bite. Don't over-explain it on screen.
