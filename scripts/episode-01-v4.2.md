## Safety and sensitivity checks
- The burn is steam, a red face and a tiny voice; cold water follows and the Uncle is fine in the next shot. The safe method (lift, spoon, tiny hole, wait, sip soup first, then the dumpling with vinegar and ginger) is shown and spoken in full in the same episode. No child copies the Uncle.
- Legend hedged out loud ("Legend says", "Many towns claim this dumpling"). Competing towns are not named on screen. No "mantou" pun etymology. No claim of thin skin in 1871.
- Qinglong line says "shipped ceramics to Japan and Korea" and avoids "earliest" or "the first port"; the pagodas are drawn small (about 11 m). No brand, no shop sign text, no Chinese text invented.
- Jokes land on the Uncle only. Grandma is always right and never a joke. Dad's scenes are warm (no jokes between 2:12 and 2:18). "Dad burned his tongue too" is a story beat.
- Fold count: the video never says any number is "official".

A warm, curious food-travel show on the old Silk Road, hosted by a boy and his (very hungry) uncle. We follow Dad's old dumpling journal, one dumpling per page.
Stop 1: Nanxiang, an old canal town now part of Shanghai, and the soup dumpling (xiaolongbao).

How does soup get inside a dumpling? It goes in as jelly, made from pork-skin stock that is boiled for hours and chilled until it sets. The cook chops the jelly into the meat, and the steam melts it back into soup. That is also why these dumplings are steamed, never boiled.
Silk Road note: the Silk Roads were a network of land and sea routes. Near Shanghai, the old port of Qinglong shipped ceramics to Japan and Korea more than a thousand years ago.
Nanxiang's two small brick pagodas are about a thousand years old. The temple around them burned in 1766 and they are still standing.
How many folds? Cooks say 14, 16 or 18. Ask three cooks, get three answers.
Legend says that around 1871 a shop in Nanxiang sold one very big meat bun, then made it smaller and added soup set into jelly. Other towns claim this dumpling too, so we say: legend.
"Life is like a soup dumpling: if you rush it, you get burned." – Dad

How to eat one safely: lift it by the top knot, put it on a spoon, make a tiny hole, wait, then sip the soup first. Then eat the dumpling with black vinegar and ginger. The soup inside is very hot.

Episode 1 of 3 in China. Next: Guangzhou and har gow, the see-through shrimp dumpling.
