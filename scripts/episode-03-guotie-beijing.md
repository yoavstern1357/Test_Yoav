## Montage style rules (same system as the old gyoza recipe)
- Fast, clear, one step per shot, top-down "cooking table" view so kids can follow.
- Every quantity appears as a big on-screen label while the step happens (read, not spoken). Hold each label at least 2 seconds.
- Safety cards use one consistent look: ⚠️ **GROWN-UP STEP** (red badge in the corner + a grown-up's hands on screen), 🧼 **RAW MEAT RULE** (blue), 🌾 **ALLERGY** (yellow). Kids' hands only appear for squeezing, mixing, folding, pinching and sauce-mixing.
- A small banner stays in the corner during the recipe: `📝 Full recipe in the description`.
- If an edit runs long, trim the Uncle gags first, **never a safety label**.

## Full recipe (for description)
Paste this whole section into the video description (it's also the printable version for the website).

```
CRISPY POTSTICKERS (GUOTIE) · about 24 · Beijing style, ends open

⚠️ GROWN-UP STEP = knives, the hot pan, hot oil and splatter, boiling water. Kids: squeezing, mixing, folding, pinching!
🧼 RAW MEAT RULE: wash hands with soap after touching raw meat, never taste the raw filling, and keep it in the fridge until you fold. Keep raw filling away from cooked food.
🌾 ALLERGY NOTE: wrappers contain wheat (gluten); the filling has soy and sesame. Check your wrapper and sauce labels.

1. THE CABBAGE (kid job: squeezing!)
- 200 g napa cabbage (about 3 leaves) · ½ tsp salt
- ⚠️ GROWN-UP STEP: chop the cabbage very finely with a knife.
- Kids: mix in the salt, wait 10 minutes, then squeeze out the water as hard as you can. Less water, less splatter!

2. THE FILLING (kid job: mixing!)
- 250 g ground pork
  · Chicken option: 250 g ground chicken
  · Vegetable option: 200 g firm tofu (pressed dry and crumbled) + 100 g mushrooms, finely chopped by a grown-up
- 2 green onions, finely chopped · 1 tsp grated fresh ginger
- 1 tbsp soy sauce · 1 tsp sesame oil · 1 tsp cornstarch · a pinch of white pepper
- Put everything in a bowl with the squeezed cabbage. Mix with a spoon or clean hands until sticky, about 1 minute.

3. FOLD, BEIJING STYLE (kid job: folding and pinching!)
- 24 round dumpling wrappers (store-bought) · a small bowl of water
- Put 1 heaped teaspoon of filling in a short line across the middle. Don't overfill.
- Wet the edge with a fingertip. Fold the wrapper in half and pinch it closed in the MIDDLE only. Leave both ends a little open: that's the classic northern guotie shape, and it's the easiest fold for kids.
- Prefer them closed? Pinch the whole edge shut and you have jiaozi. Both work!

4. COOK: FRY, STEAM, CRISP (one batch of about 12 in a 26 cm / 10-inch non-stick pan with a lid)
- ⚠️ GROWN-UP STEP (hot pan): heat 1 tbsp vegetable oil over medium heat. Line up the guotie close together.
- Fry 2–3 minutes, until the bottoms are light golden.
- ⚠️ GROWN-UP STEP (splatter!): pour in 80 ml (⅓ cup) water and cover with the lid RIGHT AWAY. It will hiss and spit. Kids stand back.
- Steam with the lid on for 5–6 minutes, until the water is almost gone.
- Take the lid off. Cook 1–2 more minutes until all the water is gone and the bottoms are deep golden and crispy.
- IS IT COOKED? ⚠️ GROWN-UP STEP: cut one open. The filling should be fully cooked with no pink, and the juices clear. With a food thermometer: pork 71°C / 160°F · chicken 74°C / 165°F. Not there yet? Add a splash of water, lid on, 1–2 minutes more.
- Repeat with the second batch.

OR BOIL THEM (jiaozi style)
- ⚠️ GROWN-UP STEP: bring a big pot of water to a boil. Slide the dumplings in gently with a slotted spoon (no splashing), and stir once so they don't stick.
- Boil about 6–8 minutes, until they float and the filling is cooked through. Check one the same way (no pink · pork 71°C / 160°F · chicken 74°C / 165°F).
- ⚠️ GROWN-UP STEP: lift them out with a slotted spoon.

5. SERVE
- ⚠️ GROWN-UP STEP: put a plate upside down over the pan and turn it over, crispy side up.
- Dipping sauce (kids can mix): 1 tbsp soy sauce + 1 tbsp Chinese black vinegar (or rice vinegar). Optional: a little grated garlic or a few drops of chili oil.
- ⚠️ Careful: the inside is very hot. Rest 1 minute before biting.

MAKE-AHEAD TIP: freeze uncooked guotie on a tray so they don't touch, then move them to a bag. Cook from frozen (don't thaw) with a little more water and a few more minutes of steaming, and always check that one is cooked through.

INGREDIENTS LIST: napa cabbage, salt, ground pork (or ground chicken, or firm tofu + mushrooms), green onions, fresh ginger, soy sauce, sesame oil, cornstarch, white pepper, round dumpling wrappers, vegetable oil. Dip: soy sauce, black or rice vinegar, optional garlic or chili oil.
```

## Notes
- **Beijing is a setting, not a birthplace.** Jiaozi and guotie are a northern-China tradition in general; no source ties guotie's invention to Beijing. The script says "Beijing style" only for the open-ended shape (common in northern guotie) and never "invented in Beijing".
- **"Legend says"** is used for Zhang Zhongjing (he was a real Eastern Han doctor, but the dumpling story is folklore). The Astana find is presented as "real history" because it is archaeological. A popular second legend (a Song-dynasty palace cook who burned boiled dumplings and called them "potstickers") is unproven and left out.
- **Midnight dumplings:** "many families in northern China", not "everyone".
- **No hidden-coin gag:** some families hide a coin in one New Year dumpling, but it's left out because of the choking risk for young viewers.
- **Safety:** all badges stay in the edit (knife, hot pan, splatter, doneness with temperatures, plate flip, "very hot inside", raw meat rule, allergy). The Uncle's "hiding from the splatter" gag is the only fear gag; Mei and the Boy model the calm, correct behaviour (step back).
- **Kids' hands** appear only for squeezing, mixing, folding, pinching and sauce-mixing.
- **Grandpa Wang and Mei** are fictional. No real restaurant, brand or person is shown.
- **Continuity:** Episode 2's red paper-cut ("Beijing. Crispy and soft.") leads here. Dad's clue is **balance** ("crispy and soft together", season map). The single 🇨🇳 stamp happens here. The chili doodle by Seoul sets up Episode 4's clue (**courage**, spicy kimchi mandu). Episode 4's opening transition still says "From Tokyo… to Seoul" and must be changed to "From Beijing… to Seoul" (season map §4); not edited here.
- **Sea road:** the boat across the Yellow Sea keeps Season 1's "ports and ships" look. No specific ferry route is named.
- **Times** are for thin store-bought wrappers and a medium flame. Thicker wrappers or a crowded pan need 1–2 more minutes of steaming; always check one is cooked through.
