# Episode 07: "Uncle vs. King Dumpling" (South Korea arc, part 1 of 3: Arrival)

**Length:** about 2:00 (1:50–2:10) · vertical 9:16 Short · **Location:** Seoul, South Korea: the city by morning, then a busy traditional food market · **Dumpling:** Mandu

## Script

**[0:00–0:03] HOOK.** Close-up: a steamer lid lifts at a market stall. Inside: one dumpling as big as a fist. UNCLE's eyes go wide.
> **UNCLE:** That's not a dumpling. That's a *pillow.*

**[0:03–0:13] THE JOURNEY CONTINUES.** Transition: the BOY opens Dad's leather **journal**. On the map, Tokyo already has its stamp. A new dotted line glows across the sea to **Seoul**. A little cartoon plane hops along the line; UNCLE is inside, asking the clouds for snacks. One fluffy cloud floats past the window. He leans close to it.
> **UNCLE:** *(to the cloud)* Excuse me… are you a steamed bun?
> **BOY (V.O.):** Dad's journal, page three. From Tokyo… to Seoul.

**[0:13–0:17] TITLE CARD.** `🥟 #07 MANDU · Seoul, South Korea`

**[0:17–0:33] ARRIVAL: SEOUL WAKES UP.** A quick, warm montage. Green mountains around the city. A palace roof with curved, painted eaves. A tall tower on a hill. A subway train glides over the river with a soft chime. Then a long covered market alley: steam clouds, golden mung-bean pancakes sizzling on wide griddles, cooks chopping, friendly shopkeepers calling out. (Keep all signs wordless or use simple food icons: no invented Korean text.)
> **BOY (V.O.):** Seoul! Mountains, palaces… and markets full of steam.
> **SHOPKEEPER:** *(warmly, to the camera)* Eoseo oseyo! Welcome, come in!

On-screen fact card: `Seoul has been Korea's capital since 1394. That's over 600 years! 🏯`

UNCLE's nose lifts into the air. He follows the smells in a wobbly zigzag: pancake griddle, soup pot, rice-cake stall. Sniff, sniff, sniff.
> **UNCLE:** For sci… *(the BOY tugs him back by his orange belt)*
> **BOY:** Mandu first, Uncle.

On-screen fact card: `Seoul's Gwangjang Market opened in 1905, one of Korea's oldest traditional markets! 🏮`

**[0:33–0:56] THE GAG.** The kind STALL OWNER sets down a plate of big steamed "king" mandu (wang-mandu). UNCLE picks up a whole one.
> **BOY:** Uncle, maybe share it…
> **UNCLE:** For science! *(stuffs the entire mandu in his mouth)*

His cheeks puff up like a giant hamster. He tries to talk. Only muffled sounds come out.
> **UNCLE:** *(muffled)* Mmf… mmmf-mmf!
> **BOY:** *(gently)* "It's delicious"?
> **UNCLE:** *(nods, tears of joy)*

**Escalation:** still chewing, UNCLE reaches for a *second* king mandu. He squeezes it in too. His cheeks grow even bigger… *BOING!* His orange belt pops off, flies through the air, and lands on top of the BOY's conical hat like a ribbon. The STALL OWNER laughs and slides over a cup of water.
> **BOY:** *(calmly, ribbon on hat)* One at a time, Uncle.
> **UNCLE:** *(muffled, holding up one finger)* Mmf. *("One.")*

**[0:56–1:18] "LEGEND SAYS…"** Storybook-style animation (paper-cut look, warm colors): a long road of camels and traders, then an old Goryeo-era street with a little shop sign shaped like a dumpling.
> **BOY (V.O.):** Many people believe mandu came to Korea about 700 years ago, in the Goryeo kingdom.
> **BOY (V.O.):** An old Goryeo song even tells of a dumpling shop!
> **BOY (V.O.):** And its name sounds like cousins far away.

On-screen fact card: `Mandu ≈ mantou (China) ≈ manti (Central Asia, Turkey) 🥟 Dumpling cousins!`

**[1:18–1:38] HEART MOMENT.** Evening. The market lanterns glow. The BOY and UNCLE sit on a low step outside the alley, very full. UNCLE ties his belt back on. He is quiet for once. The city hums softly around them.
> **UNCLE:** *(softly)* When we were little… your dad and I shared everything.
> **UNCLE:** One dumpling. Two brothers.
> **UNCLE:** He always gave me the bigger half.

The BOY smiles with his eyes closed, the way he does when he remembers Dad.

**[1:38–1:48] DAD'S WISDOM.** Soft music. A sepia memory: DAD's silhouette in a conical hat, splitting one big dumpling into pieces for many hands.
> **BOY:** Dad wrote: *"A big dumpling is too much for one… and just right for a family."*

**[1:48–2:00] BUTTON + CLIFFHANGER.** Back at the stall. One king mandu left. UNCLE slowly cuts it in half… and hands the BOY the bigger half.
> **UNCLE:** …Sharing. Got it.

The BOY turns the journal page. A small folded sketch slips out: a low table, a house with a persimmon tree… and many little hands drawn around one plate of dumplings.
> **BOY:** *(whispers)* Dad drew… a house in Seoul?
> **BOY:** *(to camera)* Dad was right… Next time: why Korean families fold mandu *together.* 👇

## Accuracy notes
- "About 700 years ago, in Goryeo" = the 14th-century, Mongol/Yuan-era theory. It is the most common view, but other sources suggest earlier arrival via the Silk Road, so the script says **"Many people believe"**.
- The **Ssanghwajeom** song (Goryeo period) is real and is usually read as describing a dumpling shop run by a foreign (probably Central Asian) shopkeeper. The exact identity of the shopkeeper is debated, so the script only says "a dumpling shop".
- The fact card uses **"≈"** (cousins), not "comes from": the mandu / mantou / manti word link is widely suggested, but the direction and origin are debated.
- **"Capital since 1394":** sources agree on 1394 (Joseon's King Taejo); Britannica notes a short break (1399–1405), so the card says "since 1394… over 600 years", not "without a break".
- **Gwangjang Market:** 1905 is the most widely cited founding year (the merchants' association dates from 1911). Sources call it "one of the oldest", not "the oldest", so the card says exactly that. The episode's stall is a generic market stall, not a specific real shop.
- **"Eoseo oseyo"** (어서 오세요) is the everyday Korean shop greeting, "Welcome, come in". Spoken only; no Korean text on screen.
- **Uncle and Dad:** the Uncle is Dad's older brother (see `scripts/README.md`), so "two brothers" in the heart moment fits the canon.
- Keep the Goryeo shop sign wordless or use a simple dumpling icon (no invented Korean text on screen). The palace, tower and subway in the montage are stylized and unlabeled.
- **Continuity:** Dad's sketch (house with a persimmon tree, many hands around one plate) leads to the Han family's home in Episode 8.
