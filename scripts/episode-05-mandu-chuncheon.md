# Episode 05: "Uncle Folds 100 Dumplings Alone" (South Korea arc, part 2 of 3: Culture)

**Length:** about 2:00 (1:50–2:10) · vertical 9:16 Short · **Location:** Grandma Han's house in Chuncheon, Gangwon (a lake city in the mountains, about an hour from Seoul by train), the evening before Seollal (Lunar New Year), then New Year's morning · **Dumpling:** Tteok-mandu-guk (mandu boiled in New Year rice-cake soup)

## Script

**[0:00–0:03] HOOK.** UNCLE sits alone behind a mountain of wrappers, folding at top speed. Flour everywhere. A wall clock spins.
> **UNCLE:** Ninety-eight… ninety-nine… *(collapses)* …help.

**[0:03–0:07] TITLE CARD.** `🥟 #05 TTEOK-MANDU-GUK · Chuncheon, South Korea`

**[0:07–0:22] THE FAMILY COMES HOME.** Quick flash: a train glides along a river through snowy hills, toward a city ringed by mountains and wide, frozen lakes. Then the front door of a cozy house slides open. Cold air and a few snowflakes blow in. Outside: a persimmon tree and the lake, just like in Dad's sketch from Episode 4. HALMEONI HAN (Grandma) steps in with the BOY and her family, just home for the holiday, everyone's arms full of bags: tofu, cabbage, green onions, more wrappers.
> **BOY (V.O.):** For New Year, families go home. Theirs is Chuncheon!
> **BOY:** Uncle… we said *wait* for everyone!
> **HALMEONI HAN:** *(laughing)* Mandu is not a race.

On-screen fact card: `Chuncheon: capital of Gangwon, the "city of lakes" 🏞️ About 1 hour from Seoul by train 🚆`

**[0:22–0:36] CULTURE MOMENT.** Wide shot: the cozy floor of a Korean home. Bright folded hanbok wait on a shelf for tomorrow. The whole HAN family sits around a low table: HALMEONI HAN (tiny, silver bun, big smile), MOM, DAD HAN and two kids, MIN-JI (8) and JUN (5). Everyone has a job: Mom mixes, Grandma folds, the kids press and pinch, Dad lines them up on trays.
> **BOY (V.O.):** Before Seollal, Korean New Year, many families make lots of mandu… together.
> **HALMEONI HAN:** Alone, it takes all night. Together? One song!

Grandma starts humming. Everyone folds to the beat.

**[0:36–0:56] THE GAG.** UNCLE joins the line. Grandma hands him one wrapper. He folds it, passes it to Min-ji, she pinches it, Jun gives it a tiny "boop" with his finger. A perfect mandu! UNCLE stares at his lonely pile of 99 lumpy ones.
> **UNCLE:** *(whispering)* …I did it wrong for an hour.
> **JUN:** *(patting his belly)* It's okay, Uncle!

**Escalation:** UNCLE tries to make up for it. He folds faster… and faster… wrappers fly into the air like snow. *Flap!* One wrapper lands flat on his own bald head. He doesn't notice.
> **UNCLE:** *(proudly)* See? Speed *and* teamwork!
> **JUN:** *(giggling, pointing)* Uncle has a hat now!

Grandma gently peels the wrapper off his head… and uses it for the next mandu.

**[0:56–1:10] NEW YEAR'S MORNING: THE BOW.** Sunlight on the snowy lake outside. Everyone in bright hanbok. Min-ji and Jun kneel and bow deeply to Grandma. She smiles and gives each of them a little envelope, which they tuck into small silk pouches.
> **BOY (V.O.):** New Year's morning, kids bow to their elders…
> **MIN-JI:** …and wish them a happy new year!

On-screen fact card: `Seollal: kids bow to elders (sebae) and receive New Year's money (sebaetdon) 🙇✨`

UNCLE bows too, very low… and gets stuck, round as a ball. Then he peeks up and holds out his hand. Grandma gives him… one mandu.
> **UNCLE:** *(delighted)* Even better.

**[1:10–1:26] TTEOK-MANDU-GUK.** In the kitchen, Grandma (a grown-up) slides the family's mandu into a big pot of gently boiling broth with white rice-cake slices. One by one, the mandu bob up to the top. Then: steaming bowls of **tteok-mandu-guk**, rice cakes and mandu in clear soup, with egg strips and green onion on top.
> **BOY (V.O.):** New Year's Day: rice-cake soup… and up here, dumplings too!
> **MIN-JI:** An old saying: one bowl makes you one year older!
> **UNCLE:** *(on his third bowl, freezes)* …Then I'm *very* old.

On-screen fact card: `In Korea's colder north (Gangwon, Seoul, Gyeonggi), many families eat dumpling soup at New Year, often with rice cakes too 🥣`

**[1:26–1:40] THE STORY MOVES.** The BOY shows Grandma Dad's little sketch: the house, the persimmon tree, the lake, the many hands. Grandma notices the BOY's conical hat. She smiles slowly and brings out an old family photo: a tall man in the **same hat**, seen from behind, sitting at this very table, folding with the family. (Sepia, no face.)
> **HALMEONI HAN:** He came one New Year. He said: "Make them together."
> **BOY:** *(whispers)* Dad…

The BOY opens the journal: under Chuncheon, a faded line glows: `Secret #5: make them together.` ✨

**[1:40–1:50] HEART MOMENT.** Night. The house is quiet. The BOY and UNCLE sit on the doorstep under the persimmon tree, the lake shining under the moon, steam rising from two cups of tea.
> **BOY:** Why did you fold alone, Uncle?
> **UNCLE:** *(quietly)* Your dad always folded with me.
> **UNCLE:** Without him… it felt wrong.

The BOY takes one wrapper from his bag and holds it out.
> **BOY:** Then fold with me.

UNCLE smiles. They fold one mandu together, four hands, very slowly.

**[1:50–2:00] BUTTON + CLIFFHANGER.** The door opens behind them.
> **HALMEONI HAN:** Tomorrow, we all go to Busan. *(pause)* I'll bring what your father left.
> **BOY:** *(eyes wide)* Dad left something?
> **BOY:** *(to camera)* Dad was right… Next: Busan, and Grandma's crispy mandu! 👇

## Notes
- **Going home for Seollal:** traveling to the family's hometown (often the grandparents' house) is a central Seollal custom, which is why the episode moves from Seoul to Grandma's house. The Han family is fictional; Grandma lives in Chuncheon, and her son's family (Mom, Dad Han, Min-ji, Jun) lives in Busan (pays off in Episode 6).
- **Chuncheon (new):** the capital of Gangwon, in a mountain basin where the Han (Bukhan) and Soyang rivers meet; it is often called the "city of lakes" (Soyang Lake, Uiam Lake). The ITX-Cheongchun train (since 2012) links it to Seoul in roughly 1 hour (about 52–75 minutes depending on the Seoul station), so the card says "about 1 hour". The train, lake and mountains are drawn stylized and unlabeled.
- **Making mandu together for Seollal:** a well-documented family tradition (often dozens or even hundreds at a time, many frozen for later). It is less common today because of busy lives, so the script says "many families", not "every family".
- **Tteok-mandu-guk:** tteokguk (sliced white rice-cake soup) is the classic New Year's Day dish; in the northern part of the peninsula (Gangwon, Seoul, Gyeonggi and North Korea), dumpling soup (manduguk) is especially traditional, often with rice cakes added (tteok-manduguk). Sources explain this by the north's colder climate, better for wheat than rice. Garnishes like egg strips and green onion are standard. The boiling is shown done by Grandma (a grown-up), with the mandu floating up when cooked.
- **"One bowl = one year older":** a traditional saying linked to the old Korean age system, where everyone turned a year older at New Year. South Korea officially uses international age since 2023, so it is framed as **"an old saying"**.
- **Sebae and sebaetdon:** on Seollal morning, children (often in hanbok) make a deep bow to elders and wish them a happy new year; elders give them New Year's money, often in envelopes, kept in a small pouch (bokjumeoni). Sources differ on details such as the number of bows, so the script shows **one deep bow** and avoids counting. In the scene, show Grandma giving white or plain envelopes (no invented Korean text), and keep the bow respectful: the only joke is the Uncle getting stuck.
- **Hanbok:** shown as festive family clothing for the morning; draw it simply and respectfully.
- **Characters:** the Han family is fictional. Show them proud and warm; all jokes are on the Uncle. Keep on-screen text in English (no invented Korean signs).
- **Dad continuity:** the old photo shows Dad's sepia silhouette only (from behind), never his face. The persimmon-tree-and-lake sketch comes from Episode 4's cliffhanger; "what your father left" pays off in Episode 6's story ending (Dad's handwritten card, in Busan).
- **Uncle and Dad:** the Uncle is Dad's older brother (see `scripts/README.md`); the doorstep scene is the arc's quiet heart moment.
- **Dialogue pace:** all sections stay at or under 3 words per second (e.g. the family-arrival beat is about 20 words in 15 s; the button is about 22 words in 10 s).
