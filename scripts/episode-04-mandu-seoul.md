# Episode 04: "Uncle vs. Kimchi Dumpling" (South Korea arc, part 1 of 3: Arrival)

**Length:** about 2:00 (1:50–2:10) · vertical 9:16 Short · **Location:** Seoul, South Korea: the city by morning, then the food alleys of Gwangjang Market · **Dumpling:** Kimchi mandu (big, steamed, folded to order at a market stall)

## Script

**[0:00–0:03] HOOK.** Close-up: a steamer lid lifts at a Gwangjang Market stall. Inside: plump kimchi mandu, each as big as a fist, the filling glowing a little orange-red through the thin skin. UNCLE's eyes go wide.
> **UNCLE:** That's not a dumpling. That's a *pillow.*

**[0:03–0:13] THE JOURNEY CONTINUES.** Transition: the BOY opens Dad's leather **journal**. On the map, **Beijing** already has its stamp. A new dotted line glows from Beijing, across the Yellow Sea, to **Seoul**. A little cartoon ferry bobs along the line; UNCLE stands on the deck, asking the clouds for snacks. One fluffy cloud floats past. He leans close to it.
> **UNCLE:** *(to the cloud)* Excuse me… are you a steamed bun?
> **BOY (V.O.):** Dad's journal, page two. From Beijing… to Seoul.

**[0:13–0:17] TITLE CARD.** `🥟 #04 KIMCHI MANDU · Seoul, South Korea`

**[0:17–0:33] ARRIVAL: SEOUL WAKES UP.** A quick, warm montage. Green mountains around the city. A palace roof with curved, painted eaves. A tall tower on a hill. A subway train glides over the river with a soft chime. Then the long covered alleys of Gwangjang Market: steam clouds, golden mung-bean pancakes sizzling on wide griddles, a cook's hands folding mandu fast, friendly shopkeepers calling out. (Keep all signs wordless or use simple food icons: no invented Korean text.)
> **BOY (V.O.):** Seoul! Mountains, palaces… and markets full of steam.
> **SHOPKEEPER:** *(warmly, to the camera)* Eoseo oseyo! Welcome, come in!

On-screen fact card: `Seoul has been Korea's capital since 1394. That's over 600 years! 🏯`

UNCLE's nose lifts into the air. He follows the smells in a wobbly zigzag: pancake griddle, soup pot, rice-cake stall. Sniff, sniff, sniff.
> **UNCLE:** For sci… *(the BOY tugs him back by his orange belt)*
> **BOY:** Mandu first, Uncle.

On-screen fact card: `Seoul's Gwangjang Market opened in 1905, one of Korea's oldest traditional markets! 🏮 Here, mandu are folded and steamed to order.`

**[0:33–0:56] THE GAG.** The kind STALL OWNER folds a fresh batch, steams it, and sets down a plate of big kimchi mandu with a little dish of soy dipping sauce. UNCLE picks up a whole one.
> **BOY:** Uncle, it's kimchi. A bit spicy…
> **UNCLE:** For science! *(stuffs the entire mandu in his mouth)*

His cheeks puff up like a giant hamster. His face slowly turns pink, then red. Tiny cartoon flames flicker above his bald head. He tries to talk. Only muffled sounds come out.
> **UNCLE:** *(muffled)* Mmf… mmmf-mmf!
> **BOY:** *(gently)* "It's delicious"?
> **UNCLE:** *(nods, happy tears)*

**Escalation:** still chewing, UNCLE reaches for a *second* kimchi mandu. He squeezes it in too. His cheeks grow even bigger… *BOING!* His orange belt pops off, flies through the air, and lands on top of the BOY's conical hat like a ribbon. The STALL OWNER laughs and slides over a cup of water.
> **BOY:** *(calmly, ribbon on hat)* One at a time, Uncle.
> **UNCLE:** *(muffled, holding up one finger)* Mmf. *("One.")*

The BOY takes one small, brave bite of his own. His eyes go wide… then he smiles.
> **BOY:** Spicy… and *so* good.

**[0:56–1:18] "LEGEND SAYS…"** Storybook-style animation (paper-cut look, warm colors): a long road of camels and traders, then an old Goryeo-era street with a little shop sign shaped like a dumpling.
> **BOY (V.O.):** Many people believe mandu came to Korea about 700 years ago, in the Goryeo kingdom.
> **BOY (V.O.):** An old Goryeo song even tells of a dumpling shop!
> **BOY (V.O.):** And its name sounds like cousins far away.

On-screen fact card: `Mandu ≈ mantou (China) ≈ manti (Central Asia, Turkey) 🥟 Dumpling cousins!`

**[1:18–1:38] HEART MOMENT.** Evening. The market lanterns glow. The BOY and UNCLE sit on a low step outside the alley, very full. UNCLE ties his belt back on. He is quiet for once. The city hums softly around them.
> **UNCLE:** *(softly)* When we were little… your dad and I shared everything.
> **UNCLE:** One dumpling. Two brothers.
> **UNCLE:** He always gave me the bigger half.

The BOY smiles with his eyes closed, the way he does when he remembers Dad.

**[1:38–1:48] DAD'S WISDOM.** Soft music. A sepia memory: DAD's silhouette in a conical hat, taking a brave first bite of something spicy, then splitting one big dumpling into pieces for many hands.
> **BOY:** Dad wrote: *"A spicy dumpling takes courage. A big one takes a family."*

**[1:48–2:00] BUTTON + CLIFFHANGER.** Back at the stall. One kimchi mandu left. UNCLE slowly cuts it in half… and hands the BOY the bigger half.
> **UNCLE:** …Sharing. Got it.

The BOY turns the journal page. A small folded sketch slips out: a low table, a house with a persimmon tree, mountains and a wide lake behind it… and many little hands drawn around one plate of dumplings.
> **BOY:** *(whispers)* Dad drew… a house by a lake?
> **BOY:** *(to camera)* Dad was right… Next time: why Korean families fold mandu *together.* 👇

## Accuracy notes
- "About 700 years ago, in Goryeo" = the 14th-century, Mongol/Yuan-era theory. It is the most common view, but other sources suggest earlier arrival via the Silk Road, so the script says **"Many people believe"**.
- The **Ssanghwajeom** song (Goryeo period) is real and is usually read as describing a dumpling shop run by a foreign (probably Central Asian) shopkeeper. The exact identity of the shopkeeper is debated, so the script only says "a dumpling shop".
- The fact card uses **"≈"** (cousins), not "comes from": the mandu / mantou / manti word link is widely suggested, but the direction and origin are debated.
- **"Capital since 1394":** sources agree on 1394 (Joseon's King Taejo); Britannica notes a short break (1399–1405), so the card says "since 1394… over 600 years", not "without a break".
- **Gwangjang Market:** 1905 is the most widely cited founding year (the merchants' association dates from 1911). Sources call it "one of the oldest", not "the oldest", so the card says exactly that. The episode's stall is a generic market stall, not a specific real shop (don't name or draw any real stall, e.g. the well-known knife-noodle stalls).
- **Kimchi mandu:** sources name meat and kimchi as the two common fillings at Gwangjang stalls, served with soy dipping sauce. The kimchi is the "spicy" part; the joke is only that the Uncle stuffs in two at once, never that the food is too hot or strange. The Boy's small bite shows it's delicious (and gives the arc's **courage** clue from `production/season-map.md`).
- **Beijing → Seoul transition:** the map shows a stylized ferry across the Yellow Sea (Season 1 is the sea road). No real ferry line or port is named, so nothing needs a schedule check.
- **"Eoseo oseyo"** (어서 오세요) is the everyday Korean shop greeting, "Welcome, come in". Spoken only; no Korean text on screen.
- **Uncle and Dad:** the Uncle is Dad's older brother (see `scripts/README.md`), so "two brothers" in the heart moment fits the canon.
- Keep the Goryeo shop sign wordless or use a simple dumpling icon (no invented Korean text on screen). The palace, tower and subway in the montage are stylized and unlabeled.
- **Continuity:** Dad's sketch (house with a persimmon tree by a lake, many hands around one plate) leads to Grandma Han's house in Chuncheon, the "lake city", in Episode 5.
- **Dialogue pace:** every spoken section stays at or under 3 words per second (the longest, the "Legend says" beat, is about 35 words in 22 s).
