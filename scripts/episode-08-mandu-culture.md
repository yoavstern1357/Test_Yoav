# Episode 08: "Uncle Folds 100 Dumplings Alone" (South Korea arc, part 2 of 3: Culture)

**Length:** about 2:00 (1:50–2:10) · vertical 9:16 Short · **Location:** the warm home of the Han family in Seoul, the evening before Seollal (Lunar New Year), then New Year's morning · **Dumpling:** Mandu (and tteok-mandu-guk)

## Script

**[0:00–0:03] HOOK.** UNCLE sits alone behind a mountain of wrappers, folding at top speed. Flour everywhere. A wall clock spins.
> **UNCLE:** Ninety-eight… ninety-nine… *(collapses)* …help.

**[0:03–0:07] TITLE CARD.** `🥟 #08 MANDU FOR LUNAR NEW YEAR`

**[0:07–0:20] THE FAMILY ARRIVES.** The front door slides open. Cold air and a few snowflakes blow in. Outside: a persimmon tree, just like in Dad's sketch from Episode 7. The BOY steps in with the HAN family, everyone's arms full of shopping bags: tofu, cabbage, green onions, more wrappers.
> **BOY:** Uncle… we said *wait* for everyone!
> **HALMEONI HAN:** *(laughing)* Mandu is not a race.

**[0:20–0:36] CULTURE MOMENT.** Wide shot: the cozy floor of a Korean home. Bright folded hanbok wait on a shelf for tomorrow. The whole HAN family sits around a low table: HALMEONI HAN (Grandma, tiny, silver bun, big smile), MOM, DAD HAN and two kids, MIN-JI (8) and JUN (5). Everyone has a job: Mom mixes, Grandma folds, the kids press and pinch, Dad lines them up on trays.
> **BOY (V.O.):** Before Seollal, Korean New Year, many families make lots of mandu… together.
> **HALMEONI HAN:** Alone, it takes all night. Together? One song!

Grandma starts humming. Everyone folds to the beat.

**[0:36–0:56] THE GAG.** UNCLE joins the line. Grandma hands him one wrapper. He folds it, passes it to Min-ji, she pinches it, Jun gives it a tiny "boop" with his finger. A perfect mandu! UNCLE stares at his lonely pile of 99 lumpy ones.
> **UNCLE:** *(whispering)* …I did it wrong for an hour.
> **JUN:** *(patting his belly)* It's okay, Uncle!

**Escalation:** UNCLE tries to make up for it. He folds faster… and faster… wrappers fly into the air like snow. *Flap!* One wrapper lands flat on his own bald head. He doesn't notice.
> **UNCLE:** *(proudly)* See? Speed *and* teamwork!
> **JUN:** *(giggling, pointing)* Uncle has a hat now!

Grandma gently peels the wrapper off his head… and uses it for the next mandu.

**[0:56–1:10] NEW YEAR'S MORNING: THE BOW.** Sunlight. Everyone in bright hanbok. Min-ji and Jun kneel and bow deeply to Grandma. She smiles and gives each of them a little envelope, which they tuck into small silk pouches.
> **BOY (V.O.):** New Year's morning, kids bow to their elders…
> **MIN-JI:** …and wish them a happy new year!

On-screen fact card: `Seollal: kids bow to elders (sebae) and receive New Year's money (sebaetdon) 🙇✨`

UNCLE bows too, very low… and gets stuck, round as a ball. Then he peeks up and holds out his hand. Grandma gives him… one mandu.
> **UNCLE:** *(delighted)* Even better.

**[1:10–1:24] TTEOK-MANDU-GUK.** Steaming bowls of **tteok-mandu-guk**: white rice-cake slices and mandu in clear soup, with egg strips and green onion on top.
> **BOY (V.O.):** New Year's Day: rice-cake soup, with dumplings!
> **MIN-JI:** An old saying: one bowl makes you one year older!
> **UNCLE:** *(on his third bowl, freezes)* …Then I'm *very* old.

**[1:24–1:40] THE STORY MOVES.** The BOY shows Grandma Dad's little sketch: the house, the persimmon tree, the many hands. Grandma notices the BOY's conical hat. She smiles slowly and brings out an old family photo: a tall man in the **same hat**, seen from behind, sitting at this very table, folding with the family. (Sepia, no face.)
> **HALMEONI HAN:** He came one New Year. He said: "Make them together."
> **BOY:** *(whispers)* Dad…

The BOY opens the journal: under Seoul, a faded line glows: `Secret #3: make them together.` ✨

**[1:40–1:50] HEART MOMENT.** Night. The house is quiet. The BOY and UNCLE sit on the doorstep under the persimmon tree, steam rising from two cups of tea.
> **BOY:** Why did you fold alone, Uncle?
> **UNCLE:** *(quietly)* Your dad always folded with me.
> **UNCLE:** Without him… it felt wrong.

The BOY takes one wrapper from his bag and holds it out.
> **BOY:** Then fold with me.

UNCLE smiles. They fold one mandu together, four hands, very slowly.

**[1:50–2:00] BUTTON + CLIFFHANGER.** The door opens behind them.
> **HALMEONI HAN:** Stay. Tomorrow we cook. *(pause)* And I'll show you what your father left here.
> **BOY:** *(eyes wide)* Dad left something?
> **BOY:** *(to camera)* Dad was right… Next: Grandma Han's family recipe! 👇

## Notes
- **Making mandu together for Seollal:** a well-documented family tradition (often dozens or even hundreds at a time, many frozen for later). It is less common today because of busy lives, so the script says "many families", not "every family".
- **Tteok-mandu-guk:** tteokguk (sliced white rice-cake soup) is the classic New Year's Day dish; adding mandu is very common, and in northern regions dumpling soup (manduguk) is especially traditional. Garnishes like egg strips and green onion are standard.
- **"One bowl = one year older":** a traditional saying linked to the old Korean age system, where everyone turned a year older at New Year. South Korea officially uses international age since 2023, so it is framed as **"an old saying"**.
- **Sebae and sebaetdon (new):** on Seollal morning, children (often in hanbok) make a deep bow to elders and wish them a happy new year; elders give them New Year's money, often in envelopes, kept in a small pouch (bokjumeoni). Sources differ on details such as the number of bows, so the script shows **one deep bow** and avoids counting. In the scene, show Grandma giving white or plain envelopes (no invented Korean text), and keep the bow respectful: the only joke is the Uncle getting stuck.
- **Hanbok:** shown as festive family clothing for the morning; draw it simply and respectfully.
- **Characters:** the Han family is fictional. Show them proud and warm; all jokes are on the Uncle. Keep on-screen text in English (no invented Korean signs).
- **Dad continuity:** the old photo shows Dad's sepia silhouette only (from behind), never his face. The persimmon-tree sketch comes from Episode 7's cliffhanger; "what your father left here" pays off in Episode 9's story ending (Dad's handwritten card).
- **Uncle and Dad:** the Uncle is Dad's older brother (see `scripts/README.md`); the doorstep scene is the arc's quiet heart moment.
