# Episode Workflow: from idea to upload

Target: **1 episode (~2 min) per week** to start (about 5–8 hours each). Consistency beats perfection.

## Step 1: Script (15 min)
Tell Claude (in Hebrew is fine) which dumpling you want, and get a script in the format in `scripts/README.md`.
Scripts for the China arc (Episodes 1–3) are ready in `scripts/`. Episode structure: `series-format.md`.

## Step 2: Images (1–2 h)
- Generate **15–20 still images** per ~2-minute episode (one per beat), using the prompts in `character-bible.md`.
- Tools (pick one): any AI image generator you have access to, Canva's built-in AI image tool, or draw your own.
- Always 9:16 vertical (1080×1920).
- Reuse your saved character reference images so the Boy and the Uncle look the same every time.

## Step 3: Motion (1–2 h)
Options, from easiest to most "animated":
1. **CapCut (free):** zoom/pan keyframes on the stills, plus shake and "pop" effects for the gags. This is enough to start.
2. **AI image-to-video tools:** turn each still into a 3–5 second moving clip, then join the clips in CapCut.
3. Later, when the channel earns: hire an animator on Fiverr for key episodes.

## Step 4: Voices (30 min)
- Easiest: record the voices yourself on your phone (in English), and pitch them up or down in CapCut for the Boy and the Uncle.
- Or: text-to-speech voices in CapCut or a voice-AI tool. Keep the **same voice per character** every episode.
- Add sound effects: steam hiss, sizzle, "TOOT TOOT", plus a soft piano for Dad's moments.
- Use only music and sounds you have rights to: the YouTube Audio Library or CapCut's licensed library.

## Step 5: Captions + export (20 min)
- CapCut → **Auto captions** (English). Use big bold text in the middle third of the screen.
- Export **1080×1920, 30fps, about 2:00** (Shorts allow up to 3:00).

## Step 6: Upload (10 min). Checklist:
- [ ] Title, description and pinned comment from the script file
- [ ] Link https://thedumplingboy.netlify.app in the **first line** of the description
- [ ] Audience: answer the **"Made for kids?"** question honestly. The show is written for a general audience (family humor, a story about loss), so "No" normally fits. But if you aim it at young children, YouTube requires "Yes", which turns off comments, memberships and personalized ads.
- [ ] **Altered or synthetic content:** YouTube asks this for realistic-looking AI content. A clearly cartoon style normally doesn't need it, but check the current wording in YouTube Studio when you upload.
- [ ] Reply to the first comments within the first hour. It helps the algorithm.

## Weekly rhythm
| Day | Task |
|---|---|
| Sun | Pick a dumpling and get the script |
| Mon–Tue | Images |
| Wed | Motion + voices |
| Thu | Captions, export, **upload** |
| Fri–Sat | Reply to comments, collect ideas for next week |

## Rules that protect monetization
- Every episode needs a **real story and script**. YouTube doesn't monetize mass-produced or repetitive AI content.
- No copyrighted music, characters or clips.
- Never buy views or subscribers.
