## Executive summary (read this if nothing else)

1. **Going general-audience is the right call, and it is legally and commercially cleaner if the show is honestly written for everyone.** Animation alone does not make a video "made for kids"; the FTC and YouTube both say an animated show can appeal to everyone. But the label is judged on the content (characters, jokes, themes, language, calls to action), and mislabeling is expensive (Disney paid $10M in 2025). The v4 shift (humor that works for adults, host format, history, travel) is exactly what supports "Not made for kids".
2. **Keep the 2:00 vertical episode as the canonical episode, but ship every episode as a family of three files:** the 2:00 Short, a genuinely re-edited 40-55 s hook cutdown that points to the full episode, and a 16:9 arc compilation (about 6 min) every third episode. Only long-form builds watch hours, and Shorts views alone will not reach the new YPP bar for a long time.
3. **Do not launch on the old calendar.** Launch only when 3 episodes meet the v4 quality bar and the next 2 are scripted and storyboarded. Recommended window: 12-19 January 2027 (Tuesday 12 Jan is the target), ramping into **Lunar New Year (Saturday 6 Feb 2027)**, the strongest seasonal dumpling moment.
4. **Money: be honest with the creator.** The 2027 YPP bar for ad revenue is 1,000 subscribers plus 8,000 watch hours (or 20M Shorts views in 90 days), and Shorts ad share additionally needs 10M qualified Shorts views per 90 days. At Shorts RPMs of roughly $0.03-0.10 per 1,000 views, even 10M views is only about $300-1,000 per quarter. The business is **sponsorships, a long-form library, Ko-fi/printables and, if it works, licensing**, not Shorts ad income. Realistic first-year income is in the low hundreds of dollars in the base case.
5. **Budget: do not buy anything until a free-pipeline test of one 20-second scene fails the creator's bar. If it fails, the best-ROI purchase is pay-per-clip or monthly image-to-video AI (Kling-class, roughly $7-37 per month, or Wan-class endpoints at about $0.06-0.10 per second), used only for the 8-12 key beats per episode (hook, gag payoffs, transitions).** Estimated $10-50 per episode. Cap at about $50 per month for the first 3 months.

### 1.2 Positioning statement (proposal, creator to approve)

> **A world food show hosted by a kid tour guide in a leaf hat, with his hopelessly hungry uncle, following Dad's journal along the Silk Road. Every episode: one dumpling, one legend, one disaster.**

- **One-line pitch:** "classic world food-travel TV, but the host is ten, the co-host is a disaster, and the legend might be true." 
- **Tagline options:** "Every dumpling has a legend. Uncle has an appetite." / "One road. One dumpling at a time." / "Dad's journal. Uncle's stomach. Your next craving."
- **Show name for browse and search:** the current series tag "Dumpling Journal" is not a phrase anyone searches. **"Silk Road" is.** Recommend a show-facing title such as **"The Dumpling Road"** (working title, check availability) with "Dad's Dumpling Journal" kept as the emotional story layer. Titles then carry a searchable phrase: "Silk Road Dumplings #1".
- **Why it can work:** the combination is rare. Animated, anime-rhythm, food, travel, history and a grief-driven mystery, in 2 minutes. The closest proof that the mix has an audience: *Delicious in Dungeon* (adventure + cooking + comedy) reached the Netflix global top 10 and was renewed (https://www.gamesradar.com/netflix-anime-series-delicious-in-dungeon-positive-reviews-2024 · https://comicbook.com/anime/news/netflixs-biggest-anime-breakout-returns-for-new-release-ahead-of-season-2/). It is a different show, but it shows that food plus anime plus comedy is a mainstream, not a niche, combination.
- **All-ages comedy lesson (from criticism of *Bluey*):** adults stay for emotional depth and for the adult characters' dry, sarcastic one-liners, not for louder kid jokes. Critics attribute its adult appeal to emotional complexity and warmth, and say the adults' wit carries much of the humor. Applied to us: let the **Uncle** carry slapstick and appetite jokes, let the **Boy** carry deadpan host narration (the straight man), and keep Dad's journal as the emotional spine. Sources: https://theconversation.com/why-are-adults-without-kids-hooked-on-bluey-and-should-we-still-be-calling-it-a-kids-show-228610 · https://collider.com/bluey-resonates-with-adults/ · https://peabodyawards.com/stories/the-cross-generational-magic-of-bluey/ (opinion and criticism, not research). The comedy-style team owns the actual joke craft; this is only the marketing implication.

### 1.4 "Made for kids" (COPPA) implications of going general audience

**What is confirmed**
- Animation alone does not make a video child-directed. FTC guidance and YouTube's own Help page say an animated program that appeals to everyone is an example of not made for kids. The deciding factors include the subject, whether children are the intended audience, and child-appealing elements such as characters, activities, songs, stories or language. Sources: https://ppc.land/youtubes-coppa-deadline-hits-what-the-audience-setting-rules-really-mean/ · https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9684541 (search-result summaries; **VERIFY** the wording on the live Help page).
- The channel-level default applies to all videos, and any video genuinely aimed at under-13s must be set to "made for kids" individually. Same sources.
- **Enforcement is real.** In September 2025 Disney agreed to pay **$10M** to settle FTC/DOJ claims that it failed to label some YouTube videos as made for kids. Civil penalties are up to **$53,088 per violation**. Sources: https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/blog/2025/09/protecting-children-watching-youtube-videos-lessons-learned-ftcs-settlement-disney · https://www.malwarebytes.com/blog/privacy/2026/01/disney-fined-10m-for-mislabeling-kids-youtube-videos-and-violating-privacy-law · https://www.finnegan.com/en/insights/articles/coppas-amended-rule-is-now-in-full-effect-what-operators-need-to-know.html
- The amended COPPA Rule took effect 23 June 2025 with a general compliance date of **22 April 2026**; it added a "mixed audience" definition and tighter consent rules for targeted ads and third-party disclosure (same Finnegan source and https://www.davispolk.com/insights/client-update/ftc-prioritizes-coppa-enforcement-new-compliance-obligations-take-effect). The search did not tie these amendments to YouTube creators specifically.
- Made for kids = comments, notifications, personalized ads, memberships, merch shelf and several other features are off (existing plan, `production/growth-plan.md` section 0).

**What this means for the show**

| Decision | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| Channel default | Set the channel to **"No, set this video as not made for kids" only if every video honestly meets that standard**; review each upload. Never use "No" to get comments or ad rates on a video that is really for young children. |
| Why v4 helps | The v4 brief (humor for adults and teens, host format, real history, travel, recipes with grown-up steps, a grief storyline) is the content-based case for "Not made for kids". The old "soft children's picture-book" look was the weak point; the v4 anime rhythm and the Silk Road mood move the audience signal the right way. The approved round characters stay, so the look still has child appeal. Content must carry the case. |
| What to avoid (hard rules) | No "kids", "toddlers", "for children" in titles, tags or descriptions; no nursery songs, counting or colour lessons; no direct address to children ("kids, ask your parents"); no child performers or child-focused CTAs; jokes and references that work for adults; recipes written to the cook (kids as helpers). |
| Keep a paper trail | Write a one-page **audience determination memo** (who it is for, what elements support that, date, who decided) and update it each season. If YouTube or a regulator ever asks, a documented good-faith decision matters. Not legal advice; a one-hour consult with a media/privacy attorney is cheap insurance before the first upload. |
| If YouTube overrides to "made for kids" | YouTube can override. The plan still works (see `growth-plan.md` section 1) but comments, memberships, Super Thanks and the merch shelf are lost, and the community tactics here move to Instagram/TikTok and the site. |
| Paid promotion | Tick "Contains paid promotion". Avoid brands that primarily target children while the show is positioned for everyone. |
| Platforms | TikTok and Instagram are 13+ platforms; write captions for teens and adults. |

## 2. Format decision

### 2.1 Is 2:00 vertical right?

**Short answer: yes as the canonical episode, no as the only deliverable.**

Facts:
- Shorts can be up to 3 minutes (square or vertical) since Oct 2024; Content-ID-claimed licensed music in Shorts over 60 s can be blocked, but our music is synthesized, so this does not apply. Source: https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15424877 (Confirmed; existing plan).
- **Views now count when a Short starts or replays, with no minimum watch time** (31 March 2025). Monetization and YPP eligibility still use **engaged views**. In Analytics, use the "engaged views" metric (Advanced mode) for real performance. Source: https://techcrunch.com/2025/03/26/youtube-is-changing-how-youtube-shorts-views-are-counted (Confirmed).
- **Shorts views do not count toward the 4,000/8,000 watch-hour route**, only toward the separate Shorts-views route (existing plan, Confirmed).
- Most third-party data favors shorter Shorts: the average Short is about 33 s, under-30 s clips are most often rated top performers in an Adobe creator survey, and Metricool found average view duration per Short fell to about 16 s. Sources: https://piktochart.com/blog/how-long-youtube-shorts/ · https://www.adobe.com/express/learn/blog/youtube-shorts-length-study · https://metricool.com/youtube-trends/ (vendor). The same sources say 1-3 min suits story and educational content but needs a very strong first few seconds. **No independent 2026 dataset compares 2-3 minute story Shorts directly with sub-60 s Shorts.** So this is an experiment to run, not a settled fact.
- **New (Sept 2026): YouTube "Shorts series".** YPP members can group Shorts (portrait or square, up to 180 s each) into seasons and episodes, with a "watch series" button, sequential playback and per-episode custom thumbnails. It rolls out on web, mobile and TV. Sources: https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/23/youtubes-new-short-series-feature-brings-episodic-viewing-to-shorts/ · https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16590581 · https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/09/24/made-on-youtube-shorts-series-microdramas/amp/ (Reported; **VERIFY** whether the early YPP tier qualifies). Our 2:00 episodes fit this feature exactly, so this is a strong reason to keep the 2:00 canonical episode and to reach YPP early.
- Mixed formats work best: AIR's analysis of 18,000 channels suggests not exceeding about 55% Shorts of total uploads before long-form audiences thin out (vendor). Sources: https://air.io/en/audience-growth/do-youtube-shorts-help-your-long-form-videos-grow-data-from-18000-channels · https://sproutsocial.com/insights/youtube-shorts-vs-long-form-content-strategy/

### 2.2 Recommended package (per arc of 3 episodes)

| Asset | Length | Purpose | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Episode (canonical)** | 1:50-2:10, 9:16 | The show. Builds the series, subscribers, the Shorts-series feature once eligible | Posted as a Short. Quality bar from the v4 brief. |
| **B. Hook cutdown** | 40-55 s, 9:16 | Discovery. Higher percent-viewed and loops; funnels to the full episode | **Truly re-edited** (new cold open or recut, one complete gag, ends on a "full episode" prompt via the Related Video link), not a trimmed duplicate, to stay clear of the repetitive-content policy. 1-2 per episode, posted 2-3 days after the episode. |
| **C. Arc compilation** | about 6 min, 16:9 | Counts for watch hours; search traffic; binge | Three episodes joined with a new intro/outro and chapters, a few days after each arc. Includes a short new host segment (a map scene) so it is not just a concatenation. |
| **D. Season compilations / specials** | 8-12 min, 16:9 | Watch hours, evergreen search, sponsor-friendly integrations | Later: "Silk Road in 20 Dumplings", Lunar New Year special. |
| **E. Recipe long-form (optional, later)** | 4-6 min, 16:9 | Search-driven watch hours | Only if the creator wants to keep recipe depth; the 2:00 recipe episode already exists. |

Watch-hour math to keep honest: 3,000 h (early tier) is 180,000 minutes; 8,000 h (full YPP 2027) is 480,000 minutes. At an average of 3 minutes watched per long-form view, that is about 60,000 and 160,000 long-form views respectively, within 12 months. That is the real size of the task. The Shorts-views route (3M in 90 days for the early tier, 20M for full YPP) is a breakout-only path.

### 2.3 Retention benchmarks (and why to set our own)

Third-party benchmarks conflict badly: one vendor puts the median Short at about 45% average view-through and the top 10% near 91%; another calls 80-95% "good". "Swipe-away under 30% in the first 3 seconds" and "50-60% of drop-offs happen in the first 3 seconds" circulate but I could not trace them to original studies. Sources: https://www.bytecap.io/research/youtube-shorts-retention-benchmarks · https://fluxnote.io/guides/good-audience-retention-youtube-shorts · https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-shorts-algorithm/ · https://dev.to/bulkdl/hook-engineering-the-science-of-the-first-3-seconds-in-short-form-video-2026-2jp9. There is no official YouTube threshold.

**Planning targets (my assumptions, to be replaced by our own data after 10 Shorts):**

| Metric (Studio, Content, Shorts) | Hook cutdown (45 s) | 2:00 episode |
|---|---|---|
| "Viewed vs swiped away" | 65%+ | 60%+ |
| Average percentage viewed | 75%+ (loops can exceed 100%) | 40-50% (about 50-60 s average view) |
| Average view duration | 30 s+ | 50 s+ |
| Likes/subscribers per 1,000 views | track, no target yet | track |

Decision rule: after 10 episodes, keep the 2:00 canonical length if the 2:00 episodes earn at least as much total watch time per episode as the cutdowns and drive more subscribers per 1,000 views. If the 2:00 episodes show a cliff before 0:45, move the first act earlier or split the episode into two 60-70 s parts.

### 2.4 The first 3 seconds and the first 30 seconds (show-specific rules)

**First 3 seconds**
1. **Frame 1 is a picture, not a logo.** No black frame, no fade-in, no studio sting. Movement in frame 1.
2. **One clear question or promise in under 8 words, on screen in the approved caption style from 0.0 s** (for example "SOUP INSIDE A DUMPLING?!").
3. **Show the Uncle mid-mistake or the Boy mid-announcement.** The two reliable openers: (a) the Uncle about to do something dumb with a dumpling; (b) the Boy delivering a deadpan host line to camera, with disaster behind him ("Welcome to Shanghai, home of the dumpling that fights back.").
4. **Sound at 0.0 s:** a punchy SFX or one spoken line. No silence, no music swell.
5. **Never start with the map or travel transition.** The map is the second beat, not the first.
6. Make one hook shot also work as the cover (see section 3).

**First 30 seconds (the contract)**
| Time | Must happen |
|---|---|
| 0:00-0:03 | Hook (above) |
| 0:03-0:08 | Host identity: the Boy introduces the dumpling and place in one line; the viewer knows "this is a show" |
| by 0:10 | First real laugh (Uncle) |
| 0:10-0:20 | Promise: what the viewer will learn or see by the end ("by the end you'll know why you never bite this") |
| by 0:25 | First "Legend says..." beat, a small curiosity, not the full story |
| by 0:25 | **Plant an open loop** (the Uncle's gag setup, Dad's journal clue, a "wait for the last page") that is paid off at 1:40-1:55 |
| every 5-8 s | New visual or joke beat (matches the fast-rhythm direction in the brief) |

Ending rules: pay off the loop, deliver the "Dad was right..." line, then a 2-second **next-stop teaser** ("Next stop...") that doubles as the loop back to frame 1.

### 2.5 Series hooks that drive binge

1. **Open the next page:** every episode ends with a journal clue that points to the next place (existing storyline).
2. **Running gags with callbacks** that only reward people who watched the earlier episodes (the Uncle's "For science!", the dumpling count, the Boy's rating card).
3. **A soft cliffhanger every third episode** (Recap and map stamp), with a map shot showing the next country lit up.
4. **A visible counter** ("Dad's Dumpling: 2 of 11 clues found") in the corner badge.
5. **Structure for the platform:** playlists (serial) per arc plus a "Start Here" playlist; once YPP-eligible, convert to a **Shorts series** (https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16590581). Put each video in 3-4 playlists, because each placement is a separate discovery surface (vendor, AIR).
6. **Link cutdowns to full episodes with the Related Video feature**, which is the only routing tool Shorts support (no end screens). The linked video must pick up where the Short left off, with no branded intro (vendor, https://www.dropmedia.co.uk/?p=8222).
7. **Do not rely on clickable links in Shorts descriptions or comments: VERIFY.** I recall (from memory, not verified this session) that YouTube made links in Shorts descriptions and comments non-clickable in 2023. If so, put the Ko-fi and site links in the **channel banner and About links**, and in long-form descriptions. The existing plan puts the link on the first line of the description; check it works before relying on it.

## 3. Packaging

### 3.1 Title rules

- Under about 60 characters; one emoji at most; no "for kids"; one capitalized word is fine.
- Include a searchable noun phrase (the dumpling name, the city, "Silk Road") plus a curiosity or conflict, plus the Uncle.
- Keep the series number at the end (`#1`) so the sequence is visible in search and the channel grid. For Shorts, vary titles across episodes and compare results, because YouTube's title/thumbnail A/B test is reported not to apply to Shorts thumbnails (https://ppc.land/youtube-ends-2-year-wait-for-shorts-thumbnails-but-blocks-a-b-testing/).

**Title formulas for a general audience**
1. **Rule + violation:** `Never Bite a {Dumpling}. Uncle Did.`
2. **Hidden thing:** `The {Soup} Is INSIDE the {Dumpling}?!`
3. **Legend with doubt:** `The Legend of the {Dumpling} (Is It True?)`
4. **How to, with a cost:** `How to Eat {Dumpling} Without {Disaster}`
5. **Place + show tag:** `{City}: The Dumpling That {Surprising Verb} | Silk Road #{n}`
6. **Dad's journal:** `Dad's Journal, Page {n}: {Clue}`
7. **Rating gag (later, recurring):** `Uncle Rates Every {Country} Dumpling`
8. **Search-first (compilations):** `Every Silk Road Dumpling Explained | {Arc}`

### 3.3 Cover and first-frame rules for Shorts

Facts:
- **Custom Shorts thumbnails** began rolling out on 25 July 2026 for **YPP members only, on desktop, with no A/B testing**; without YPP, desktop users can pick from three suggested frames. Shorts thumbnails do **not** appear in the Shorts feed; they appear in search, channel pages, subscriptions and the home feed. Sources: https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/07/24/youtube-shorts-custom-thumbnails-neal-mohan-feature-update/ · https://ppc.land/youtube-ends-2-year-wait-for-shorts-thumbnails-but-blocks-a-b-testing/ (Reported). Image size guidance conflicts across sources; plan for 1080x1920 and **VERIFY in Studio**.
- Implication: before YPP we cannot upload a cover, so **the cover is whichever of the three suggested frames YouTube offers**. We must therefore make the whole first half-second thumbnail-worthy and make a second thumbnail-worthy frame appear in the first few seconds.

Rules:
1. **Frame at 0.0-0.5 s is the cover frame**: a face, big, with a readable expression; one dumpling visible; 2-4 words of text inside the middle third (top and bottom are covered by the app UI).
2. **Same corner badge on every episode** (a small passport stamp with the episode number). It builds recognition in the channel grid and shelf.
3. **High-contrast warm palette** from the style constants (leaf yellow, cream, orange, peach, brown) against one cool accent (Silk Road teal or lapis) so thumbnails stand out in a feed of red and orange food content.
4. **Check at phone-thumbnail size** (about 120 px wide) before publishing. If the expression is not readable, the frame fails.
5. **The Uncle's face is the brand**; the Boy's hat and ribbon silhouette is the logo. Use both in every cover.
6. **No clutter**: one subject, one object, one word group.

## 4. Launch plan

### 4.1 How many episodes before launch

There is no platform rule and no strong public data on the optimal buffer, so this is judgment:

- **Minimum to launch: 3 finished episodes at the v4 quality bar (the China arc, Ep 1-3) plus 2 more with locked scripts and storyboards.** Three episodes make a complete arc, allow a same-week binge, and let a new visitor see a show, not a single video.
- **Do not launch with the old v3 look.** The creator's verdict was that only the captions were right. A weak first impression cannot be re-launched. Launch with the best three.
- **Why not launch with 10:** the animation method is still being decided; a large buffer made with a method that gets replaced is waste. The 3 + 2 rule limits that risk.
- **Hard gate (my recommendation):** the first 30 seconds of Episode 1 pass the hook test in action 1 below with people outside the creator's circle.

### 4.2 Timing

- **Target launch: Tuesday 12 January 2027** (earliest sensible; later if the quality gate is not met). Reasons: it gives a buffer for the new animation method; it lands in the weeks before **Lunar New Year, Saturday 6 February 2027** (https://publicholidays.com/lunar-new-year/; one municipal page lists 7 Feb, most sources 6 Feb), when dumpling interest peaks; and there is nothing to gain by launching in the dead week between Christmas and New Year, when creators and viewers are away.
- **The Feb 2027 YPP change does not favor launching earlier.** We cannot reach 1,000 subscribers plus 4,000 hours (or 10M Shorts views) before 31 Jan 2027 starting from zero, so the 2027 thresholds are the planning baseline (see section 5).
- **Seasonal plan:** a Lunar New Year special (a dumpling-at-New-Year episode or compilation, 4-6 Feb). Verify the custom with two sources before scripting, per the accuracy rules.

### 4.3 Cadence

- **Launch week:** Ep 1 and Ep 2 on day 1 (Tuesday, 18:00 local), Ep 3 on Thursday. Same-day pair so a viewer who likes Ep 1 can immediately continue.
- **Then 1 canonical episode per week as the floor, 2 per week as the target**, plus 1-2 cutdowns per episode. The existing golden rule stands: a schedule you can keep beats a schedule that looks impressive. At v4 production cost, one per week is realistic.
- Third-party guidance is 3-5 Shorts a week as a common sweet spot, and 35+ Shorts a month was associated with weaker long-form performance (industry blogs, https://metricool.com/youtube-trends/). Our canonical plus cutdown plan lands at 8-12 Shorts a month, which is fine.
- **Never post two canonical episodes the same day after launch week.**
- Arc compilation: 3-5 days after each arc's third episode.

Several "reach penalty" numbers online are unsourced; ignore them.

### 4.5 Community tactics

1. **Pinned comment with a question that has two obvious answers** ("Sip first or bite first?"). Existing pattern is good.
2. **"Where next?" votes** limited to bonus episodes (the route is fixed by Dad's map). Credit the viewer handle only with consent; **never feature or credit children**.
3. **Invite correction, politely.** Each episode's pinned comment asks natives to share how their family says it or eats it. Diaspora viewers become the most loyal commenters and the best accuracy check. Respond to corrections publicly and pin the best.
4. **Pre-launch teaser series (2 weeks):** 3-4 posts of 10-20 s ("Dad's journal, page torn"), character sketches, a map reveal. Goal is only 100-300 followers on TikTok/Instagram so launch day has a few real viewers, not buying anyone.
5. **Cultural partners per arc:** one friendly food account or home cook from each country (a comment exchange or a shout-out, not a paid deal) for each arc. Respect first; no one should feel mocked.
6. **Genre communities, used carefully:** r/dumplings-type subreddits, anime-food and food-history communities, and teacher groups for the Silk Road. Follow each community's self-promotion rules; post value (a short fact or the recipe), not a bare link.
7. **First hour after each upload:** reply to every comment (on MFK = No videos), pin, heart the best. Existing checklist.
8. **Never buy views, subscribers or engagement.** It poisons the data and risks the channel.

### 6.3 Cost per episode (my estimate, built from per-second prices above)

Assumptions: a 2:00 episode needs about 40-60 s of true character motion generated by AI (hook, gag payoffs, key transitions); the rest uses the existing pipeline (camera moves, layers, captions, held shots). Vendors and independent write-ups say the real cost is per **usable** second after retries; I assume 3-4 generations per usable second (https://tech-insider.org/?p=46247 and https://magichour.ai/blog/ai-video-pricing-index discuss this effect, vendor).

| Route | Per-second price | Cost for 50 usable s at 3.5 retries (175 generated s) |
|---|---|---|
| Veo 3.1 Lite | $0.03-0.05 | $5-9 |
| Wan 2.2/2.6 endpoints | $0.06-0.10 | $11-18 |
| Runway Gen-4.5 | $0.12 | $21 |
| Kling 3.0 Pro via API | $0.22 | $39 |

So **about $10-40 per episode**, **$40-160 per month at 4 episodes**, before a human voice. Treat these as a budget range, not a quote. Subscription plans (Kling Standard to Pro) are a cheaper way to experiment than the API but credits expire monthly.

### 6.4 When to buy: decision rules

1. **Wait for the free test.** The video-tech teammate's free-repo result on a 20-second scene decides. Also note the **Canva video quota returns 2026-12-16** (brief); use that free quota before paying.
2. **Buy only if the free result fails the creator's bar.** Then run a **one-week paid test**: one month of the cheapest Kling-class plan (about $7-10) or $15-20 of pay-per-clip credit, the same 20-second scene, the same reference sheet, three vendors if possible. Judge line stability, face and hat drift, and flat cel shading, not demo reels.
3. **Cap spend:** about **$50 per month for months 1-3**, funded by the creator, not by revenue (there is none yet). Raise it only if one of: sponsor revenue, 500+ subscribers with rising retention, or a clear quality jump that moves the 2:00 episode's average percentage viewed.
4. **Order of upgrades by expected ROI:** (1) image-to-video motion for the hook and gag beats; (2) a better voice for the Uncle; (3) a human-drawn cover and trailer; (4) music/SFX library; (5) everything else. Skip paid promotion until organic data shows an episode that already retains.
5. **Do not buy** subscriber or view services, bulk "faceless channel" software, or annual plans before the 3-month test.
6. **The Ko-fi goal** ("$100 for animation tools") is a good public story, but do not count on it to fund tools. Tips at this stage will not.
