# Quality research: what makes an animated Short "high quality" (and how we measure it)

Written: 2026-11-26 · Role: research analyst · Status: research and recommendations only. Nothing was uploaded, posted, committed or pushed.
Scope: what viewers and platforms reward, the craft standards professionals work to, what viewers criticize in AI animation, and benchmark channels. It ends in a measurable rubric for our episodes and a list of what we are probably doing wrong, given the defects the creator confirmed on Ep 1 v4.4 (duplicated characters, smeared faces, desync).
Read with: `production/v4/PRODUCTION-BIBLE.md`, `production/v4/pro-upgrade-plan.md`, `production/v4/marketing.md`, `production/v4/motion-tech.md`.

## (a) Executive summary

1. **The first 1-3 seconds decide distribution, and the platform gives you a direct gauge for it.** YouTube Studio's Shorts metric "Viewed vs. swiped away" is the hook-health number. YouTube publishes **no official benchmark** for it, so compare each episode with the channel's own baseline. Practitioners reshoot the first 2 seconds rather than the middle when that number is weak. [Standard/Vendor] https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12220281 · https://contentstudio.io/blog/youtube-shorts-analytics-guide · https://1of10.com/blog/youtube-shorts-analytics/
2. **Our master runs at 30 fps, but our animation runs at 12 or 24 fps. That uneven cadence is a technical defect professionals would spot at once.** [Repo: `build_spec_ep01v44.py` sets `fps=30`; `motion-tech.md` delivers clips at 12 fps "on twos".] 12 drawings per second on a 30 fps timeline means each drawing is held for 2 or 3 frames, alternating, which reads as judder. Animation is built on a 24 fps timeline: characters on twos or threes, mixed by scene, while camera moves and effects can run on ones. [Community/Reported] https://AnimeNewsNetwork.com/encyclopedia/lexicon.php?id=61 · https://animetudes.com/2020/05/17/animation-and-subjectivity-towards-a-theory-of-framerate-modulation/ · YouTube asks you to upload at the native frame rate: https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1722171
3. **"Smoother" does not mean "better" for anime-style animation.** Fans and critics push back hard on AI-interpolated anime: it looks blurry and the impacts lose punch. Interpolation tools are built for live action and ghost on big pose changes. Holding drawings on twos should be a deliberate per-shot choice, not a side effect of a broken RIFE pass. [Reported/Community] https://www.avclub.com/ai-brained-sickos-who-upscale-anime-to-60-fps-need-to-be-stopped · https://gamerant.com/why-anime-doesnt-work-in-60-fps/ · https://www.animenewsnetwork.com/bbs/phpBB2/viewtopic.php?p=4869299
4. **Studios keep characters on-model with model sheets, and the AI creators who succeed do the same with locked references.** Model sheets exist because many hands draw one character; anything that departs from them is "off-model". AI practitioners say consistency comes from reference images and per-character LoRAs, not longer prompts. They also say multi-character contact and overlapping bodies are the top failure point (blended limbs, swapped identities, a character rendered twice). [Reported/Vendor] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Model_sheet · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Off-model · https://invideo.io/faq/why-do-ai-video-models-fail-when-two-characters-interact/ · https://higgsfield.ai/blog/two-characters-one-scene-ai-video · https://dev.to/qcrao/character-consistency-in-ai-comics-3-tricks-that-beat-lora-training-for-me-3ad7
5. **Lip-sync has hard published tolerances.** ITU-R BT.1359 puts the threshold where people notice A/V offset at about **+45 ms (audio early) to -125 ms (audio late)**. Wikipedia reports EBU R37 at **+40 / -60 ms** end to end (I could not open R37 itself). At 24 fps one frame is 41.7 ms, so we should keep **audio no more than 1 frame early and 2 frames late**. [Standard] https://www.itu.int/dms_pubrec/itu-r/rec/bt/R-REC-BT.1359-1-199811-I!!PDF-E.pdf · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Audio-to-video_synchronization
6. **Anime and Western animation sync dialogue in opposite orders, and ours fits neither.** In anime, the animation is made first, mouths are simple open/close flaps, and actors record to the picture. In Western animation, dialogue is recorded first and an animator maps mouth shapes to it frame by frame (at least an "ah" and an "oo" shape). We generate silent clips and lay TTS on top, so nobody times the mouths to the voice. Our TTS is cheap to re-run, so the Western "pre-lay" order (voice first, animatic timed to the audio) fits us better. [Reported/Community] https://www.animenewsnetwork.com/answerman/2017-12-11/.125115 · https://www.animenewsnetwork.com/bbs/phpBB2/viewtopic.php?p=4934718
7. **What viewers call "AI slop" in animation is specific:** floaty motion with no weight, morphing bodies and faces, shots that don't match each other, garbled text, mouths that "yell" with no matching face, and an overall feeling of being soulless. Big budgets do not fix it: Coca-Cola's 2025 AI ad reportedly took 70,000 prompts and was still mocked for shots that don't match and poorly animated animals. [Reported/Community] https://www.techradar.com/ai-platforms-assistants/coca-cola-faces-backlash-again-for-missing-the-spirit-of-the-holidays-with-another-ai-slop-ad · https://www.creativebloq.com/news/ai-animation-needs-to-stop · https://amp.knowyourmeme.com/news/ai-generated-anime-goes-viral-on-social-media-largely-because-of-people-who-think-it-stinks · https://www.opus.pro/blog/ai-slop-aesthetic-12-tells
8. **AI creators who are respected keep the authorship human.** Neural Viz writes its scripts by hand, storyboards, and has the creator perform every character through performance capture (Runway Act-One and Act-Two), with tools to keep lighting and eyelines consistent. YouTube's July 2026 policy clarification demonetizes generic, repetitive, template-made content, not AI as such. [Reported] https://streamingmeme-main.vercel.app/articles/the-future-of-ai-isnt-just-slop-wired (mirror of a WIRED profile) · https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/07/13/youtube-inauthentic-content-monetization-policy-update/ · https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/20/youtube-clarifies-policies-around-ai-slop-and-upsetting-videos/
9. **Our sound is about 2 dB quiet and sits under the platform UI.** We master to -16 LUFS / -2 dBTP [Repo: `make_episode.py` loudnorm]. The common target is -14 LUFS integrated with a -1 dBTP true peak. YouTube turns loud videos down but does **not** turn quiet ones up, so a -16 mix plays quieter than its neighbours in the feed. [Vendor/Community, consistent] https://productionadvice.co.uk/stats-for-nerds/ · https://productionadvice.co.uk/youtube-loudness/ · Our caption band can sit as low as 88% of the frame height (centre at 1,690 px), inside the area where Shorts, TikTok and Reels draw their buttons and text (roughly the bottom 310-480 px). [Vendor, numbers disagree] https://adaptlypost.com/en/blog/social-media-safe-zones-2026-complete-guide · https://wildandfreetools.com/blog/vertical-video-safe-zones-guide-2026/
10. **Successful animated channels win on a clear premise, recurring characters and quality per upload, not volume.** OverSimplified has about 9.5M subscribers from roughly 33 videos, often a year apart. Kurzgesagt spends about 1,200 hours per video. YouTube's 2026 animation report says 63% of 14-24-year-old animation fans watch series made for YouTube weekly or more. The reality check for our niche: an AI-look 2D cooking channel (Indian Ghibli Kitchen) reached about 26.6K subscribers with 153 videos in about 11 months. [Reported/Vendor] https://becomeviral.com/blog/oversimplified-case-study · https://kottke.org/20/02/how-to-make-a-kurzgesagt-video-in-just-1200-hours · https://blog.youtube/culture-and-trends/independent-animation-trends-report/ · https://vidiq.com/youtube-stats/channel/@indianghiblikitchen/about/index.md

### 1.2 Technical craft standards

**Frame rate and "on twos/threes"**
- At 24 fps: on ones = 24 drawings per second, on twos = 12, on threes = 8. Anime mixes rates even inside one scene (ones for fast action or effects, twos and threes for acting and holds). The higher-budget work is mostly on twos. [Community/Reported] https://AnimeNewsNetwork.com/encyclopedia/lexicon.php?id=61 · https://www.idtech.com/blog/what-does-animating-on-ones-twos-and-threes-mean · https://wavemotioncannon.com/2016/12/31/an-introduction-to-framerate-modulation/
- Frame-rate changes carry meaning: the animator goes to ones when a moment must feel fluid or important. [Community] https://animetudes.com/2020/05/17/animation-and-subjectivity-towards-a-theory-of-framerate-modulation/
- YouTube: upload at the native frame rate; H.264 High profile, closed GOP, 4:2:0, about 8 Mbps at 1080p for standard frame rates. [Standard] https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1722171
- **Applied to us:** a 24 fps master. Character clips held on twos (each drawing exactly 2 frames) or delivered at 24. Camera moves, shake and caption animation run on ones. Never 12 fps into 30.

**Character consistency, model sheets and acting**
- Model sheets (turnaround, expressions, poses, hands) keep many artists on-model. Deliberate "off-model" drawing is a comedy tool for big emotion, chibi and sweat-drop gags, but only when it is chosen on purpose. [Reported] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Model_sheet · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Off-model
- Even the 2023 Corridor "AI anime" was criticized for not understanding that "anime characters aren't actually meant to look exactly the same all the time". In other words, acting deformation must be designed, while identity has to stay fixed. [Reported] https://screenrant.com/anime-rock-paper-scissors-ai-art-controversy/ · https://www.animenewsnetwork.com:443/feature/2023-03-15/no-you-cant-make-anime-with-ai/.195921
- The acting basics come from the 12 principles (anticipation, staging, follow-through, secondary action, timing, appeal). Animators' main complaint about AI motion is that it ignores them: "no weight, no timing, no interesting spacing", "fills space with motion but does not know why the character moves". [Reported/Community] https://www.studiobinder.com/blog/what-are-the-12-principles-of-animation/ · https://www.creativebloq.com/news/ai-animation-needs-to-stop · https://discussions.reallusion.com/t/ai-doesnt-have-to-replace-talent/15939
- Impacts: a single distorted "impact frame" at contact plus a camera bump in the direction of the hit; smears turn fast limbs into readable arcs. Wide shots set up distance and wind-up, then cut to medium shots for the impact. [Community] https://jbsiraudin.github.io/blog/demon-slayer-visual-grammar · https://fullfrontal.moe/sakuga-espresso/page/3

**Lip-sync**
- Tolerances: BT.1359 detectability is +45 / -125 ms and acceptability +90 / -185 ms. EBU R37 is +40 / -60 ms (via Wikipedia). For film the frame accuracy is about ±22 ms. [Standard] https://www.itu.int/dms_pubrec/itu-r/rec/bt/R-REC-BT.1359-1-199811-I!!PDF-E.pdf · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Audio-to-video_synchronization
- Anime convention: open/close flaps (often 3 mouth positions) timed to the line's start and end, not to phonemes. Viewers accept that if the flaps start and stop on the line and the mouth is shut during silence. Western convention: phoneme-mapped mouth shapes from pre-recorded dialogue. [Reported/Community] https://www.animenewsnetwork.com/answerman/2017-12-11/.125115
- Limited-animation tricks for hiding mouths: still shots used as rhythmic pauses, cinematic angles and detailed single shots. Camera movement is rare, which gives it more weight when it happens. [Reported] https://www.animenewsnetwork.com/answerman/2018-01-31/.127150 · https://www.animenation.net/blog/?p=1593
- Open audio-driven lip-sync models: **LatentSync** (Apache-2.0, the quality pick, about 18 GB VRAM for v1.6). **MuseTalk** (MIT code; the README flags the bundled dependencies and test data; about 4 GB VRAM; some jitter). **Wan2.2-S2V-14B** and **InfiniteTalk** (Apache-2.0, audio-driven, full body). **None is documented on anime or cartoon faces**, so test before relying on any of them. [Vendor/primary repos via snippet] https://github.com/bytedance/LatentSync · https://github.com/TMElyralab/MuseTalk · https://huggingface.co/Wan-AI/Wan2.2-S2V-14B · https://github.com/MeiGen-AI/InfiniteTalk · https://www.creativeainews.com/articles/open-source-lip-sync-licenses-commercial-use-2026/
- Automatic measurement: SyncNet's offset search (±10 frames) and the LSE-C/LSE-D scores. There is no official pass mark, and the scores correlate only weakly with human judgment. They are trained on real faces, so on anime faces they are a hint at best. [Research] https://arxiv.org/pdf/2008.10010 · https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.06421

**Camera language and editing**
- 180-degree rule: in a dialogue, characters keep their screen sides and their eyelines must match across cuts. Crossing the line flips the geography and disorients the viewer, so fix the axis at the storyboard stage. [Reported] https://www.studiobinder.com/blog/what-is-the-180-degree-rule/ · https://nofilmschool.com/actor-gaze-180-degree-rule · https://www.studiobinder.com/blog/what-is-screen-direction-in-film/
- AI-camera tells: drift with "floatiness that doesn't match human-held cameras" and slow motion by default. Diffusion models prefer slow, smooth motion because it is easier to keep consistent. Fix: specific action verbs and explicit camera speed in the prompt; for exact moves, add the camera in the edit rather than in the prompt. [Vendor, LTX's own blog] https://ltx.io/blog/how-to-fix-slow-motion-in-ai-generated-video · https://ltx.io/blog/camera-movement-prompt-guide · https://ltx.io/blog/ltx-2-3-prompt-guide
- LTX image-to-video guidance: describe the motion, not what is already in the image; use one take per first frame; prefer medium-to-wide, centred framing over extreme close-ups that need fine detail. [Vendor] https://ltx.io/blog/ltx-2-3-prompt-guide · https://www.veed.io/learn/ltx-video-prompting-guide

**Color**
- Plan a color script per scene, grade one "hero frame" per location and match the other shots to it, with a repeatable LUT. For AI clips, repeat the palette words word for word in every prompt and colour-match the clips in the edit, because each model grades differently. [Reported/Vendor] https://www.studiobinder.com/blog/what-is-a-color-script-definition/ · https://studio.blender.org/blog/color-scripting/ · https://blog.cg-wire.com/color-correction-animation · https://hackernoon.com/how-to-color-match-ai-video-clips-when-every-model-grades-differently

**Sound design, mixing and loudness**
- Targets: -14 LUFS integrated and ≤ -1 dBTP true peak is the most-cited target for YouTube, TikTok and Reels. TikTok and Meta publish no official figure, and some sources say -16 or even -9 to -12. YouTube's "Stats for nerds" shows the content-loudness offset from its reference. YouTube only turns down. [Vendor/Community] https://productionadvice.co.uk/stats-for-nerds/ · https://www.forasoft.com/learn/audio-for-video/articles-audio/lufs-targets-per-platform-2026 · https://opus.pro/blog/best-loudness-normalizers
- Mixing for phone speakers: one main element at a time, usually the voice. Phones lose low end, wide stereo and quiet textures, and music is the most common masker. Mix in stems (dialogue, ambience, effects, music) and test on several speakers. People overrate how intelligible their own dialogue is, because they already know the lines. [Community/Course] https://cursa.app/en/page/sound-design-that-reads-on-small-speakers · https://eprints.bournemouth.ac.uk/36627/1/BFI%20Sound%20Design%20%26%20Mixing%20Handbook.pdf
- Foley carries character believability and ambience defines the space. [Reported] https://binus.ac.id/bandung/dkv/2025/11/04/using-sound-to-enhance-animated-stories/
- Voices: raters penalize **both** flat and overacted delivery, and flat monotone is the most common complaint. AI voices can land a beat late, which kills a joke. [Reported/Vendor] https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/416978/ · https://air.io/en/audience-growth/how-to-expand-reach-with-ai-dubbing-without-sounding-robotic

**Captions**
- Safe zones disagree by vendor: TikTok keeps the bottom 320-480 px, the right 120 px and the top 108 px clear. Reels: top 210, bottom 310, right 84. Shorts: about 984x1500 usable. The shared recommendation is a centred 900x1400 box. Preview in each app. [Vendor] https://adaptlypost.com/en/blog/social-media-safe-zones-2026-complete-guide · https://reap.video/blog/short-form-video-safe-zones · https://wildandfreetools.com/blog/vertical-video-safe-zones-guide-2026/
- Line length and reading speed: Netflix uses 42 characters per line for Latin-alphabet languages. Several Netflix language guides cap reading speed at 17 characters per second for adult shows and 13 for children's (the English figure was not in the snippets). [Standard] https://backlothelp.netflix.com/hc/en-us/articles/219375728 · https://backlothelp.netflix.com/hc/en-us/articles/115002812588-Slovak-Timed-Text-Style-Guide
- Word-by-word highlighted captions are reported as critical for food Shorts because so much viewing is silent. [Vendor] https://fluxnote.io/blog/youtube-shorts-strategy-for-cooking-creators-in-2026

## (b) Measurable quality rubric (per episode, both orientations)

**How to use it:** run the automatic checks on every clip before the edit (clip gate) and on the final render (render gate). Then do one human pass on a contact sheet at 4 frames per second of every shot. **CRITICAL** items must pass 100%. Every other item must pass in at least 90% of shots. A failed clip is re-rolled once (GPU budget rule), then fixed by staging (cutaway, wider shot, still hold).
Thresholds marked [Assumption] are starting points: calibrate them on the approved stills and on the first 3 episodes, then freeze them.

## (c) The 10 things we are probably doing wrong

Ordered by how much each one explains the creator's confirmed defects (duplicated characters, smeared faces, desync), and then by viewer impact. "Evidence in repo" means I checked our files; the causes are hypotheses to confirm by testing.

1. **We ask one AI clip to hold two or three characters with no separate anchors, so the model clones one of them.** (Duplicated characters.) Two-shots and Grandma scenes are generated as single clips from multi-reference stills, and the literature names overlapping bodies and unanchored identities as the main cause of blending and cloning. **Fix:** check the head count on the still *before* it reaches the GPU (rubric C1). Give one acting character per generated clip where possible, and build two-shots as composited layers (character clip over a background plate). Name the exact count in the prompt ("exactly one boy and one large man, no other people") and use a negative prompt (duplicate, twin, clone, extra person, crowd). Regenerate from a corrected still instead of re-rolling the same one.
2. **Faces are too small and too low-resolution at generation, and the camera zooms in by itself, so faces smear.** (Smeared faces.) Evidence in repo: `motion-tech.md` says "without a camera sentence it zooms in and the uncle's face smears", and clips are generated at 480x832 and then upscaled 2.25x. LTX's own guidance warns against extreme close-ups that need fine detail. **Fix:** always include a camera sentence ("static camera" or a named move). Generate close-ups as medium shots and punch in during compositing (at most 1.3x). Re-roll any clip that fails rubric D1. For talking close-ups, use a still-based face layer plus mouth shapes instead of a generated face.
3. **We generate silent clips and lay the TTS on top, so no mouth is timed to any voice.** (Desync.) Evidence in repo: pro-upgrade audit item 1. Neither the anime order (picture first, actors record to the flaps) nor the Western one (voice first, mouth shapes mapped to it) is being followed. **Fix:** adopt pre-lay. Render the TTS first, lock an animatic to the audio (gate 2), then generate each clip at the length of its line. Prompt "mouth closed, listening" for non-speakers and "talking" only for the speaker. Run a lip-sync pass only on close-ups (license-checked: LatentSync Apache-2.0 first, tested on our anime faces). Stage everything else per rubric E3.
4. **The master is 30 fps while the animation is 12 or 24 fps, so the cadence judders.** Evidence in repo: `build_spec_ep01v44.py` sets `fps=30`, and the renderer resamples clips to that rate. 12 fps on 30 fps alternates 2-frame and 3-frame holds, and 24 on 30 has a 3:2 judder. **Fix:** a 24 fps master. Hold character clips on clean twos (exact 2-frame holds) or deliver them at 24. Run camera, shake and captions on ones (rubric B1, B2, F5).
5. **"On twos" is the side effect of a broken RIFE pass, not a choice.** Evidence in repo: HANDOFF says RIFE x2 corrupts frames, so every 2nd frame is dropped. Fans dislike interpolated anime, and RIFE ghosts on big poses anyway. **Fix:** decide per shot. Use ones (native 24 fps generation) for fast action and food money shots (steam, soup pour), and twos for acting and dialogue. Use RIFE only on small motion, gated by rubric B3.
6. **We use AI clips past their stable window and ask them to make big pose changes.** Evidence in repo: `motion-tech.md` says 4 s clips freeze or drift at the end, and the stable acting happens in the first 2 s. Drift and melting grow with clip length and with the amount of change. **Fix:** use 2-3 s clips with one action each, trim the tail (D2), use start and end keyframes for gags, and cut between clips instead of stretching one.
7. **Captions can land under the platform UI.** Evidence in repo: `safe_caption_y` searches down to 0.88 of the height in portrait (default 0.80, spec `caption_y=0.8`). Its centre at 1,536-1,690 px sits inside the bottom 310-480 px that TikTok, Reels and Shorts cover. **Fix:** clamp the caption box to rubric H1 (bottom edge ≤ 1,440 px), keep it clear of the right 120 px, and preview with a safe-zone overlay.
8. **The mix is quieter than the feed and thin on foley.** Evidence in repo: `loudnorm=I=-16:TP=-2` (and a mono mobile file). YouTube does not boost quiet uploads, and the audit says foley is minimal. **Fix:** -14 LUFS / -1 dBTP (I1). Ducking so music sits about 10 dB under the dialogue (I3). A foley pass on every bite, step and steam hiss (I5). Stems. Check on a phone speaker (I4).
9. **An identical ~11.5 s anime opening plays before the episode's own hook in a Short.** Evidence in repo: `make_opening_cuts.py` writes an about 11.5 s `opening_intro.wav`, and the bible puts the series intro at the start of every episode. That spends the swipe-decision window on a repeated sequence, and repeated openings add to the "repetitive content" signal. **Fix:** Shorts order is a 3-8 s cold open from the episode, then a ≤ 3-4 s title sting, then the story (A3). The full opening stays in the long-form arc compilations and in a separate music Short.
10. **There is no per-clip quality gate, no model sheet and no character LoRA, so drift is only caught by the creator.** Evidence in repo: pro-upgrade audit items 2 and 6, and Grandma drifts the most. Studios prevent this with model sheets; AI series with per-character LoRAs and locked references. **Fix:** build model sheets (turnaround, 12 expressions, hands) and train LoRAs for the Boy, the Uncle and Grandma (pro-upgrade track A). Automate rubric B5, B7, C1, C2, D1 and E2 as a clip gate with one automatic re-roll, then a human contact-sheet pass before any render goes to the creator.

Also worth fixing (outside the top 10): flat TTS comedy timing (pick takes per line, and time pauses to the script, E5), a camera that is mostly static or floaty (D6, F5), and no color grade (G1).
