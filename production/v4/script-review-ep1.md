## 2. Saturated vs fresh in our concept
| Element | Verdict | Why / what to do |
|---|---|---|
| Fat, greedy Uncle, steam from the ears, ice pack | Saturated (cartoon staple) | Works only through voice and timing; keep it short, never the only joke. |
| "Rule one... don't..." then the gag | Familiar structure, fresh wording | The Boy states the rule, the Narrator counts down; the deadpan "...do that." is the twist. |
| Trailer-voice Narrator on tiny stakes ("The stall of DESTINY") | Common comedic device, fresh mix | Fresh because paired with a real food fact and a legend scroll. |
| Mystery book of Dad / missing parent | Saturated hook in kids' and family stories | Fresh here only because it is gentle and unresolved: the book is a real travel guide to real food, the answers arrive one dumpling at a time. |
| Dumpling battles / rival | Saturated (cooking-contest shows) | Not in 1A/1B; keep for later arcs. |
| Legend scroll | Common in history Shorts | Fresh as a 6.5 s paper-cut gag with a hedge ("LEGEND SAYS!"). |
| Japanese punk opening | Fresh (rare in food/travel Shorts) and a risk | Strong brand, but 7.5 s of non-story near second 4 can cost swipes; premiere exception only. |
| "The soup goes in SOLID" | Genuinely fresh, shareable fact | The "wait, really?" beat is the best share trigger in the pair. |
| One dumpling per episode + real sources + safe-eating badge | Fresh and trust-building | Counter to slop: named sources, grown-up badges, "Legend says". |
| Patience payoff "nine seconds / it was four" | Fresh, quotable | Good comment and share material. |
| Camel as a recurring easter egg | Fresh, cheap | Added a paper camel to the 1A legend so 1B's camel pays off (rewatch hunt). |

## 4. Changes made (only where a score rose)
**1A**
1. K04 narration: "Around 1871, a shop shrank its giant bun... hiding jelly inside!" to "Around 1871, a shop made its bun tiny... hiding jelly inside!" (no tongue twister; 11 words, same count; fits 6.5 s). Spine and laugh map text updated.
2. K04 edit: tiny paper camel crosses the shop doorway (code sprite, about 1 s); laugh map entry added (rewatch and cross-episode payoff).
3. Pinned comment replaced (one clear question: how did you think the soup got in; camel P.S.); loop idea and a one-line share prompt added to the upload section and description.
4. Recommendation (no script change, premiere exception stays): choose title option 1 (the question) so the feed shows the topic during the 7.5 s opening; if analytics show a drop at 0:04-0:11, later re-uploads or the Shorts-feed copy may trim the opening; the 16:9 compilation keeps it.

**1B**
1. C06 Grandma: "Eighteen folds, each by hand. No hurry." to "The waiting seat. Eighteen folds, by hand." It finally answers the Boy's question, makes the stool rule understandable on mute, and sets up "A man sat there once" and "He waited all afternoon". Uncle's "I'll... perish." still lands.
2. C15 Boy: "The answers are all in his book." to "The answers are in his book." (word budget).
3. Loop idea and Shorts series grouping added to the upload section. Word count 114 to 113 (inside 108-114); lint PASS.

Kept as is: both hooks, the whole Dad beat (6.0 s, 10 s margins), "Dad was right...", the catchphrase, bald Uncle with curled mustache, no secret page, all clip timings.

## 5. Residual risks
- The 7.5 s opening in 1A (second 3.5 to 11) has no English story content; swipe risk is real. Judge it on "Viewed vs. swiped away" for 1A against 1B.
- 1B's quiet middle (0:25-0:51) will probably dip in retention; it is the emotional core and the creator's rule. Watch the retention curve there.
- The 1A pinned comment promises nothing; the creator should reply to early comments (replies help engagement) but that is a manual step.
- Market numbers are unofficial and unverified; nothing here predicts results.
- The 18-fold count is widely cited, not a standard (stays hedged in the description).
- Voice and animation quality (smeared faces, desync) still decide whether the jokes land; this review covers scripts only.
